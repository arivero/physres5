                                                                                    EMILIO A. LAURET

                                                 Abstract. We give an explicit description of the spectrum of the Hodge–Laplace operator on
                                                 p-forms of an arbitrary lens space for any p. We write the two generating functions encoding
                                                 the p-spectrum as rational functions. As a consequence, we prove a geometric characterization

                                                 of lens spaces that are p-isospectral for every p in an interval of the form [0, p0 ].




                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                             Each compact Riemannian manifold (M, g) has an associated Hodge–Laplace operator ∆p
                                          acting on smooth p-forms given by dd∗ + d∗ d, where d denotes the exterior derivative and
                                          d∗ denotes the formal adjoint. Here ∆0 is the Laplace-Beltrami operator. The spectrum of
                                          ∆p is discrete and is usually called the p-spectrum of (M, g), denoted by Specp (M, g) or just
                                          Specp (M). Two compact Riemannian manifolds will be said to be p-isospectral if their p-spectra
                                          coincide and in the literature, one abbreviates 0-isospectral to isospectral. It is well known that
                                          Specp (M, g) does not determine the geometry of (M, g), as shown by many examples of non-
                                          isometric p-isospectral manifolds, via the so called generalized Sunada method (see for instance
                                          [Mi64], [Vi80], [Su85], [DG89]) working for all p and also with methods working for individual
                                          values of p (see for instance [Go86], [Ik88], [Gt00], [MR01], [GM06]).
                                             Lens spaces are compact manifolds with positive constant curvature and cyclic fundamen-
                                          tal group. This class of spaces has provided many isospectral examples of different kinds.
                                          Ikeda [Ik80] gave families of lens spaces mutually 0-isospectral (see also [Sh11]). Later in [Ik88],
                                          using the same family, he found pairs of lens spaces that are p-isospectral for all 0 ≤ p < p0 but
                                          not p0 -isospectral, for any p0 > 0. More recently, Miatello, Rossetti and the author [LMR16a]
                                          found families of pairs, in any odd dimension n ≥ 5, of lens spaces that are p-isospectral for
                                          all p, but are not strongly isospectral. In particular such pair cannot be constructed by the
                                          generalized Sunada method due to DeTurck and Gordon [DG89], which uses representation
                                          equivalent discrete subgroups.
                                             Furthermore, [LMR16a] also gives an explicit formula for the multiplicity of each eigenvalue
                                          in the 0-spectrum of a lens space and a geometric characterization of 0-isospectral lens spaces.
                                          The one-norm k·k1 of an element in Zn is given by the sum of the absolute values of its entries,
                                          and two subsets L and L′ of Zn are called k·k1 -isospectral if for each positive integer k, there
                                          are the same number of elements in L and in L′ with one norm equal to k. Each (2n − 1)-
                                          dimensional lens space L has associated a congruence lattice L ⊂ Zn (see (2.5)). Hence, the
                                          above mentioned characterization for 0-isospectral lens spaces can be stated as follows: L and
                                          L′ are 0-isospectral if and only if L and L′ are k·k1 -isospectral. (See [BL17], [LMR16b], [La16],
                                          [MH17], [MH16] for related results.)
                                             The aim of this paper, as a continuation of the study begun in [LMR16a], is to give an
                                          explicit description of the p-spectrum of a lens space for any value of p (Theorem 3.3). As a
                                          consequence, for any p0 ≥ 0, we obtain a geometric characterization of lens spaces p-isospectral
