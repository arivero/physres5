\section{Theorem targets for a derivation}
\label{app:theorem-targets}
% Status: open. Collects theorem targets, source-backed constraints, and proof obligations.

This appendix states the analytical obligations in theorem form.  Each target
names its hypotheses, conclusion, current evidence, and failure mode.  A
physical derivation proves one of the targets below, replaces it with a
stronger statement, or demotes the associated claim.

\subsection{Common format}
\label{app:theorem-format}

The targets share a common data structure.  Let \(\mathcal S\) be a source theory or effective description.  It may be a four-dimensional electroweak theory, a boundary effective action from an interval compactification, an open-string endpoint sector, or a localized \(G_2\) system.  Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be the two-dimensional light subspace that is meant to carry the order-parameter and current amplitudes.  The desired reduction has the form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_J
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal M_J
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  0 & \sqrt J\\
  \sqrt J & -J
  \end{pmatrix}
  \longrightarrow
  \{x_+(J),x_-(J)\}.
  \label{eq:appendix-reduction-chain}
\end{equation}
The reduction is meaningful after the normalization of \(\mathcal H_J\) and the scheme in which the resulting eigenvalues are read have been specified.  The pole-observable section fixes the electroweak comparison as a complex-pole statement, with Breit-Wigner/on-shell and running quantities treated as translated inputs \cite{MartinRobertson2025,CDFII2022}.  The electroweak section fixes the low-energy field-theory data that any assignment theorem must respect \cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018,AlvarezGaumeVazquezMozo2023}.  The string and Kaluza--Klein sections list candidate sources of the two-channel determinant \cite{TongString,Polchinski1994,CsakiHubiszMeade2005,SalamStrathdee1982}.

For each target below, ``proof'' means an explicit derivation from a stated action, boundary condition, operator algebra, or compactification mode equation.  A plausibility argument may motivate the target, yet the manuscript's final physical claim must track the proven statement.

\subsection{Target 0: unified source-to-pole matching}
\label{app:target-unified-source-to-pole}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Let \(r\in\{\mathrm{end},\mathrm{int},G_2\}\) label an endpoint,
interval, or localized-singularity source description \(\mathcal S_r\).  The
source theory supplies a two-dimensional light subspace
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_{J,r}=\operatorname{span}\{h_{J,r},a_{J,r}\},
  \qquad
  \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_r,
  \qquad
  P_{J,r},
  \label{eq:target0-light-space}
\end{equation}
where the inner product and projection map \(P_{J,r}\) define the reduced
field basis.  Extra light channels are absent, integrated out, or
block-diagonalized by the same projection.  The route reduction has Schur form
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  =
  K^{\rm bdry}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  -
  V_{J,r}^{\dagger}(\lambda-L_{J,r})^{-1}V_{J,r}.
  \label{eq:target0-schur}
  \end{equation}
For the interval/CHM realization, Target 0 includes the neutral admissibility
lemma of Sec.~\ref{sec:kk-schur-target}.  The source route must prove positive
projected CHM norms, photon-orthogonality preservation, the projector-order or
commutation condition, photon-reference subtraction, the shared
\(\Lambda_{\rm CHM}\), and the kinetic-slope condition
\begin{equation}
  Z_J^{\rm cur}
  =
  \partial_{\widehat\lambda}\widehat K_J^{\rm cur}(0)>0 .
  \label{eq:target0-chm-current-slope}
\end{equation}
The comparison with \(J\) uses the canonical current entry obtained by dividing
by \(Z_J^{\rm cur}\).

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
In the reduced basis of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target0-light-space},
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda&-\sqrt J\\
  -\sqrt J&\lambda+J
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:target0-devries-kernel}
\end{equation}
The same field basis carries the electroweak sampling maps
\begin{equation}
  P_W:\ h_{J,r}\mapsto C_2(\mathbf 2)=\frac34,
  \qquad
  P_Z:\ a_{J,r}^{\gamma^\perp}\mapsto C_2(\mathrm{adj})=2,
  \qquad
  P_\gamma:\ a_{\gamma,r}\mapsto 0,
  \label{eq:target0-ew-projectors}
\end{equation}
and the source kernel contributes to the four-dimensional transverse
self-energy in the pole scheme,
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Pi^{(4)}_{T,V}(s;J,r)
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J,r)=0.
  \label{eq:target0-pole-chain}
\end{equation}
Thus one theorem supplies the source reduction, the reduced inner product, the
DeVries entries, the ordered W/Z sampling maps, and the pole-placement chain.
For the CHM electroweak realization, the sampling maps have the refined form
\begin{equation}
  P_W(h_{J,r})=\frac34+\Delta_W^r,\qquad
  P_Z(a_{J,r}^{\gamma^\perp})=2+\Delta_Y^r,\qquad
  P_\gamma(a_{\gamma,r})=0,
  \label{eq:target0-ew-projector-remainders}
\end{equation}
with \(\Delta_W^r=\Delta_Y^r=0\) as the zero-remainder outcome in the selected
source convention.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The endpoint, interval, and \(G_2\) sections supply candidate arenas for the
three route types.  The interval route has the most explicit boundary
operator language, while the \(G_2\) route supplies localization language for
gauge and matter data.  The unified theorem is the common acceptance test
shared by the routes.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the source theory yields extra coupled light channels, a
route-dependent inner product, a different trace or mixing product, an
unordered electroweak sampling map, or a high-scale quantity whose matching to
complex poles changes the determinant.  Such an outcome classifies the source
as a neighboring spectral construction.

\subsection{Target 0a: admissibility filters for route reductions}
\label{app:target-admissibility-filters}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume a proposed source route supplies a reduced two-channel matrix, a parent
representation route for the ordered \(J\)-assignment, or an SO(32)-flavor
boundary sector.  Let \(Q(J)\), \(K_{\rm seed}^{(1)}\), and
\(K_-^{\sigma_3}\) denote distinct reduced matrices until a common source
operator, projection, field basis, and normalization identify their vector
spaces.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The route must pass three filters before it can support the DeVries pole-ratio
claim:
\begin{enumerate}
\item any trace, determinant, or coefficient transfer among \(Q(J)\),
  \(K_{\rm seed}^{(1)}\), and \(K_-^{\sigma_3}\) is derived from a common
  source operator;
\item any Wigner--Eckart or Clebsch derivation specifies the parent
  representation, operator tensor type, projection, and normalization;
\item any SO(32)-flavor sector states its extra-state projection, anomaly and
  hypercharge checks, global-form compatibility, and electroweak-kernel
  coupling.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The trace-space separation follows from the fact that the matrices above act
on different reduced variables in the present draft.  The natural
Wigner--Eckart trials have been tested at the level needed for route triage:
the doublet-parent route is removed by the selection rule, and the triplet
axial route gives a parent-Casimir datum different from the DeVries target.
The SO(32) sector supplies representation bookkeeping and a possible string
boundary arena.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails for a route if a coefficient is imported across trace spaces,
if the ordered \(J\)-assignment depends on a parent representation already
excluded by the selection rule or parent-Casimir datum, or if flavor
bookkeeping is used before the completion checks are supplied.

\subsection{Target I: pole placement}
\label{app:target-pole-placement}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume a source theory \(\mathcal S\) gives a two-branch spectral equation with roots \(x_\pm(J)\).  Assume the electroweak comparison is made with transverse gauge-boson complex poles,
\begin{equation}
  \det\Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V)=0,
  \qquad
  s_V=M_{V,\rm pole}^2-iM_{V,\rm pole}\Gamma_{V,\rm pole}.
  \label{eq:target-complex-pole}
\end{equation}
Assume the mass and width symbols are in the pole parameterization of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.  If an input uses the alternative
parameterization \(s_{\rm pole}=(M-i\Gamma/2)^2\), first translate it to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.  If W/Z inputs are quoted in a common
variable-width Breit--Wigner line-shape convention, convert
\begin{equation}
  M_{V,\rm pole}
  =
  \frac{M_{V,\rm BW}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_V^2}},
  \qquad
  \Gamma_{V,\rm pole}
  =
  \frac{\Gamma_{V,\rm BW}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_V^2}},
  \qquad
  \gamma_V=\frac{\Gamma_{V,\rm BW}}{M_{V,\rm BW}} .
  \label{eq:target-bw-width-map}
\end{equation}
This gives
\begin{equation}
  M_{V,\rm BW}^2=M_{V,\rm pole}^2+\Gamma_{V,\rm pole}^2 .
  \label{eq:target-bw-mass-map}
\end{equation}
Assume common experimental mass conventions are converted into the pole convention before forming
\begin{equation}
  \sPole=1-\frac{M_{W,\rm pole}^2}{M_{Z,\rm pole}^2}.
  \label{eq:target-spole}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The theorem target is a pole-spectrum identification:
\begin{equation}
  \sPole
  =
  1-\frac{x_+(J_H)}{x_+(J_{\rm adj})}
  +\Delta_{\rm match}
  \label{eq:target-pole-conclusion}
\end{equation}
with a stated matching remainder.  The theorem either derives
\(\Delta_{\rm match}=0\) in the pole scheme or computes the controlled
radiative, threshold, and scheme contribution.  The theorem must also explain
why the assigned scale is the low-energy pole spectrum, with high-scale
boundary placements handled by a separate derivation.
For any compactification, endpoint, interval, or local \(G_2\) source, the
scale-placement statement has the matching form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  K_J^{\rm bare}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Gamma_{\rm eff}[H,W,B]
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s)
  \big|_{s=s_{W,Z}} .
  \label{eq:target-pole-matching-chain}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is the source reduction.  The second arrow is four-dimensional
effective matching.  The third arrow is the dressed transverse pole condition.
The pole reading requires all three arrows in one scheme.  Target~0 packages
this requirement with the reduced field basis and electroweak sampling maps.
Equivalently, the source kernel must produce the relevant pole self-energy
term,
\begin{equation}
  K_J^{\rm bare}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Pi_{T,V}^{(4)}(s;J),
  \qquad
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J)=0,
  \label{eq:target-self-energy-chain}
\end{equation}
with the same pole parameterization and field basis used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The manuscript already separates complex poles, Breit-Wigner/on-shell masses, \(\MSbar\) running angles, effective weak angles, GUT boundary values, and compactification eigenvalues.  Martin--Robertson supply the scheme language used in Sec.~\ref{sec:pole}.  The CDF-II result has descriptive displacement status here.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the derived clean value lives at a high-scale boundary, a running \(\MSbar\) angle, an effective leptonic angle, or a compactification eigenvalue with a different translation law.  In that case the pole-ratio reading becomes a motivating clue, and the manuscript relocates the physical statement to the derived scheme and scale.  A compactification-scale or unification-scale derivation would still be valuable; it would convert the DeVries quotient into a boundary datum whose relation to W/Z pole data is a separate running, threshold, and matching problem.

\subsection{Target II: electroweak assignment}
\label{app:target-electroweak-assignment}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Let \(H\) be the Standard Model Higgs doublet and let \(J_H=3/4\) be its \(SU(2)_L\) quadratic invariant.  Let \(J_{\rm adj}=2\) be the corresponding adjoint invariant for \(SU(2)_L\) currents.  The gauge-Higgs sector contains the usual mass terms
\begin{equation}
  M_W^2=\frac{g^2v^2}{4},
  \qquad
  M_Z^2=\frac{(g^2+g'^2)v^2}{4},
  \qquad
  M_\gamma^2=0,
  \label{eq:target-ew-masses}
\end{equation}
with the custodial structure and \(\rho=1\) tree-level relation discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ew-embedding}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
Target II states the low-energy electroweak mass-map data and the ordered
invariants.  Target~\ref{app:target-pole-placement} carries complex-pole
matching and scheme conversion.  The required O1 theorem is the existence of a
source package
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D_{\rm EW}:
  (H,J^a_\mu,B_\mu)
  \longmapsto
  \left(
  \mathcal H_J,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\Lambda_J,
  P_W,P_Z,P_\gamma,\mathcal R_{\rm pole}
  \right),
  \label{eq:target-ew-map}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  P_W(h_J)=J_H=\frac34,\qquad
  P_Z(a_J^{\gamma^\perp})=J_{\rm adj}=2,\qquad
  P_\gamma(a_\gamma)=0.
  \label{eq:target-ew-projectors}
\end{equation}
The pole-level conclusion is then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{M_{W,\rm pole}^2}{M_{Z,\rm pole}^2}
  =
  \frac{x_+(J_H)}{x_+(J_{\rm adj})}
  +\Delta_{\rm O1}.
  \label{eq:target-ew-conclusion}
\end{equation}
The derivation must say why the charged comparison samples the
order-parameter invariant and why the neutral comparison samples the
adjoint/current invariant.  The allowed mathematical channels for the charged
sample are: a Higgs-representation contribution inside the gauge-Higgs mass
map, a scalar contribution to the \(W\) transverse self-energy, or a
source/boundary label coupled to the Higgsing field.

The acceptance conditions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the same \(\mathcal H_J\), inner product, and \(\Lambda_J\) support
  \(h_J\) and \(a_J^{\gamma^\perp}\);
\item \(P_\gamma\) fixes the photon null reference;
\item the neutral projector \(P_Z\) is defined after the \(\gamma^\perp\)
  projection in the \(W^3\)--\(B\) sector;
\item \(\Delta_{\rm O1}\) is derived in the pole scheme or retained as a
  stated source remainder;
\item the negative branch uses the same source package through the scalar
  functional recorded in Target~\ref{app:target-negative-branch}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The Higgs doublet and adjoint invariants are fixed representation-theoretic facts.  The electroweak mass matrix, photon null direction, custodial relation, and projective reading of \(g:g'\) are standard low-energy data \cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018,AlvarezGaumeVazquezMozo2023}.  CHM interval sources supply boundary kinetic terms, vector Robin data, scalar \(A_5/\pi_i\) equations, custodial boundary data, and photon-zero-mode bookkeeping \cite{CsakiHubiszMeade2005}.  Coquereaux supplies the generalized-connection grading vocabulary for a gauge-Higgs source complex \cite{Coquereaux1992Superconnections}.  The manuscript has converted the assignment into a theorem target.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the assignment uses observed vector spin or an arbitrary post-selection of \(J\) values.  A successful derivation must arise from the gauge-Higgs variables, a boundary operator coupled to them, or a compactification mechanism whose low-energy reduction yields the ordered pair.

Targets~\ref{app:target-electroweak-assignment} and
\ref{app:target-negative-branch}.  The surviving result would then be a Hodge
factorization analogy for the off-diagonal entry.

\subsection{Claim hierarchy and acceptance criteria}
\label{app:claim-hierarchy}

The theorem targets separate four kinds of statements:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}}
\toprule
Level & Manuscript content & Required evidence\\
\midrule
Derived & algebra of \(Q(J)\), roots, branch identities, trace and determinant form & equation-level derivation in Sec.~\ref{sec:casimir}\\
Source-supported & electroweak mass matrix, pole/running scheme distinctions, endpoint/brane, KK, \(G_2\), and Hodge/SUSY-QM background & cited local source fragments and references\\
Conjectural & pole placement of the DeVries quotient, negative-branch scalar reading, route-specific kernel entries, broken Hodge/SUSY-QM reduction & theorem target with assumptions and failure mode\\
Open & ordered \(J\)-assignment, electroweak-ray admissibility, full determinant derivation, scalar functional, global-form compatibility of a compact completion, sourced \(B_J\) breaking term & proof from a stated action, boundary condition, endpoint algebra, localized operator, or reduced supersymmetric quantum mechanics\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A minimal derivation has the chain
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_J
  \longrightarrow
  K_J(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  (J_H,J_{\rm adj})
  \longrightarrow
  (\sPole,\mathcal F_{\rm sc}).
  \label{eq:appendix-minimum-viable-derivation}
\end{equation}
If Target~\ref{app:target-alpha-endpoint} is added, the derivation has a
conditional corollary
\begin{equation}
  (\sPole,\mathcal F_{\rm sc},U(1)_{\rm em})
  \longrightarrow
  \alpha_{\mathcal S}(Q_\alpha),
  \label{eq:appendix-alpha-corollary}
\end{equation}
with the weak-angle and threshold remainders of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target-alpha-theta-matching} and
\eqref{eq:target-alpha-matching}.
If Target~\ref{app:target-regge-intercept-survival} is added, the source theory
must also supply the intercept corollary
\begin{equation}
  K_j(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  M^2_{N_{\rm osc},j,\sigma}
  =
  \mu^2x_\sigma(j(j+1))+\frac{N_{\rm osc}}{\alpha'}
  +\Delta^{\rm Regge}_{N_{\rm osc},j,\sigma}.
  \label{eq:appendix-regge-corollary}
\end{equation}
The source theory \(\mathcal S\) may be a boundary effective action, an
endpoint sector, a localized \(G_2\) system, or a Hodge/SUSY-QM reduction.  The light subspace
\(\mathcal H_J\) must contain an order-parameter channel and an adjoint/current
channel.  The kernel \(K_J(\lambda)\) must reduce to the DeVries determinant in
a stated normalization.  The ordered pair \((J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)\) must
arise from gauge-Higgs or source-theory data.  The final arrow must attach the
positive branch to the pole observable and the negative branch to a defined
scalar functional.  The same derivation must preserve the electroweak ray of
Target~\ref{app:target-electroweak-ray}.

The common acceptance criterion is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \det K_J(\lambda)&=\lambda^2+J\lambda-J,\\
  M_\gamma^2(t)&=0,\quad
  \frac{M_W^2(t)}{M_Z^2(t)}
  =\frac{x_+(J_H)}{x_+(J_{\rm adj})}
  +\Delta_{\rm O1}^{\rm ray}(t),\\
  (J_W,J_Z)&=(3/4,2),\\
  u_-(J)&\mapsto\mathcal F_{\rm sc},\\
  K_J^{\rm bare}
  &\to\Pi^{(4)}_{T,V}(s;J)
  \to\Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J)=0 .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:appendix-acceptance-criterion}
\end{equation}
Partial results still sharpen the construction when they prove one arrow in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-minimum-viable-derivation}.  An interval model may
derive the determinant class, an endpoint model may derive the ordered
representation pair, and a \(G_2\) model may supply a localization mechanism.
Each result changes the status of a corresponding theorem target.

\subsection{Acceptance conditions}
\label{app:derivation-checklist}

The theorem targets define the following acceptance conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item State the source theory \(\mathcal S\) and the light subspace \(\mathcal H_J\).
\item Specify the normalization that makes \(\Sigma_{hh,J}=0\), \(\Sigma_{aa,J}=J\), and \(\Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J\).
\item Derive the ordered assignment \((J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)\) from low-energy gauge-Higgs or boundary data.
\item Prove ray admissibility: one radial electroweak parameter, persistent photon null direction, and simultaneous W/Z mass collapse.
\item Give the pole-scheme translation for every quoted W/Z mass input.
\item Identify the negative branch as scalar data, auxiliary data, or a derived sector with another physical role.
\item If the electromagnetic endpoint is used, derive the \(U(1)_{\rm em}\)
gauge kinetic normalization, the scalar/vector normalization constants, and
the matching pair \((Q_\alpha,\Delta_{\alpha,{\rm match}})\).
\item If the Regge-intercept extension is used, separate the DeVries sector
label \(j\), the oscillator level \(N_{\rm osc}\), and the physical trajectory
spin, then derive the projection rule for the \(j=3/2\) positive intercept.
\item If the branch-scaling interpretation is used, derive
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-branch-scaling-table}, the negative-branch mass reading,
and the boundary-condition duality map from the source theory.
\item If the Hodge/SUSY-QM route is used, derive
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-susyqm-red-block} from normalized \(0/1\)-form channels,
the source of \(J\), the finite projection, the breaking operator \(B_J\), and
the same pole/scalar maps used in the electroweak acceptance criterion.
\item Check global-form, anomaly, and representation compatibility.
\end{enumerate}
These conditions define a complete derivation for the present manuscript.
