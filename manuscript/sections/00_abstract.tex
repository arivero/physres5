The Rivero--de Vries electroweak observation is formulated as a pole-spectrum mass-ratio problem organized by a two-branch secular equation.  The algebraic input inserts \(J=s(s+1)\), historically a quadratic-Casimir eigenvalue, into \(x^2+Jx-J=0\).  The positive-root quotient \(1-x_+(3/4)/x_+(2)=0.22310132\ldots\) is proposed for comparison with the W/Z complex-pole mass-ratio observable \(\sPole\), with the low-energy pole spectrum taken as the working placement hypothesis.  The negative branch belongs to the same diagonalization and is treated as a candidate scalar/order-parameter datum whose physical meaning requires a gauge-invariant scalar functional.  Endpoint, interval Kaluza--Klein, and localized \(G_2\) settings supply candidate arenas for a two-channel kernel; the central theorem targets are the ordered assignment \((J_W,J_Z)=(3/4,2)\), the derivation of the DeVries kernel entries, and the pole-matched scalar interpretation of the partner branch.
