\section{Analytical targets and acceptance tests}
\label{app:theorem-targets}
% Status: open. Condenses the source-theory obligations used by the main text.

The paper uses the DeVries relation as a conditional pole-spectrum proposal.
The algebra in Sec.~\ref{sec:casimir} is exact.  The physical claim requires a
source theory that supplies the field basis, inner product, normalization, pole
map, and decoupling of extra channels.  A minimal source package has the form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D:
  \hbox{source data}
  \longmapsto
  \left(\mathcal H_J,K_J(\lambda),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_J,
  \mathcal R_{\rm pole}\right),
  \label{eq:appendix-source-package}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal H_J=\operatorname{span}\{h_J,a_J\}\) is the reduced
order-parameter/current space.  The reduced kernel must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  K_J(\lambda)=
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda+\Sigma_{hh,J} & \Sigma_{ha,J}\\
  \Sigma_{ah,J} & \lambda+\Sigma_{aa,J}
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:appendix-kernel-target}
\end{equation}
The entry conditions are
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{hh,J}=0,\quad
  \Sigma_{aa,J}=J,\quad
  \Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J .
  \label{eq:appendix-kernel-entries}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:appendix-kernel-target} is the source-theory version of
the two-channel operator \(Q(J)\).  Every route below is tested against this
same kernel target.

\subsection{Pole placement}
\label{app:target-pole-placement}

The pole-placement target is a theorem connecting the reduced source kernel to
the complex pole observable
\begin{equation}
  \sPole=1-\frac{M_{W,\rm pole}^2}{M_{Z,\rm pole}^2}.
  \label{eq:appendix-pole-target}
\end{equation}
The theorem must specify the convention map from any Breit--Wigner or
on-shell input to the complex pole, and it must identify the matching remainder
\(\Delta_{\rm match}\) in
\begin{equation}
  \sPole
  =
  1-\frac{x_+(3/4)}{x_+(2)}
  +\Delta_{\rm match}.
  \label{eq:appendix-pole-remainder}
\end{equation}
The exact low-energy statement requires \(\Delta_{\rm match}=0\) in the
chosen pole scheme.  A high-scale, running, or compactification placement is a
different source statement with its own running and threshold map.

\subsection{Electroweak assignment and ray admissibility}
\label{app:target-electroweak-assignment}
\label{app:target-electroweak-ray}

The ordered electroweak assignment is
\begin{equation}
  (J_W,J_Z)=(J_H,J_{\rm adj})=\left(\frac34,2\right).
  \label{eq:appendix-ew-assignment}
\end{equation}
A derivation must produce this order from gauge-Higgs, endpoint, boundary, or
compactification data.  It must also preserve the electroweak vacuum ray:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  M_W^2(v)=\frac{g^2v^2}{4},\\
  M_Z^2(v)=\frac{(g^2+g'^2)v^2}{4},\\
  M_\gamma^2(v)=0 .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:appendix-ew-ray}
\end{equation}
The admissible deformation has one radial parameter \(v\), a fixed photon null
direction, and a fixed projective W/Z direction before source-theory matching.

\subsection{Local route kernel}
\label{app:target-boundary-determinant}
\label{app:target-g2-ade-pairing}

Endpoint, interval, and localized \(G_2\) routes are acceptable only after
they produce the entries in Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-kernel-target} from a
single source problem.  The required data are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a two-dimensional light subspace \(\mathcal H_J\);
\item a physical inner product and normalization scale \(\Lambda_J\);
\item a projection that removes photon and extra-channel directions;
\item a pole map from the source spectral parameter to W/Z pole positions;
\item a calculation of \(\Sigma_{aa,J}\) and
\(\Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}\).
\end{enumerate}
For an interval route, the source objects may be a Dirichlet-to-Neumann kernel,
brane terms, scalar boundary equations, and a Schur complement.  For a
localized \(G_2\) route, the source objects may be singular-locus gauge data,
critical-point wavefunctions, flow-line bilinears, and compact-completion
constraints.  In either case, trace and determinant statements stay inside the
reduced source matrix until a common operator identifies the relevant vector
spaces.

\subsection{Partner branch and scalar data}
\label{app:target-negative-branch}
\label{app:target-alpha-endpoint}

The negative root belongs to the same diagonalization as \(x_+(J)\).  A scalar
interpretation requires a gauge-invariant functional
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_{\rm sc}(u_-(J);J)
  \label{eq:appendix-scalar-functional}
\end{equation}
with a specified normalization, scheme, and source meaning.  The functional may
be a Higgs-potential parameter, a boundary scalar, a brane-separation mode, a
Wilson-line datum, or a compactification deformation.  If the source theory
assigns \(u_-(J)\) to an auxiliary sector, the pole-ratio proposal can survive
while scalar claims are removed from the physical conclusion.

The electromagnetic coupling \(\alpha\) is a separate endpoint target.  A claim
for \(\alpha\) requires the same source package to fix the \(U(1)_{\rm em}\)
generator, charge lattice, scalar normalization, matching scale, and threshold
map.

\subsection{Additional filters}
\label{app:target-branch-scaling}
\label{app:target-regge-intercept-survival}
\label{app:target-susyqm-origin}

Higher branches, Regge placement, and Hodge/superconnection square-root
mechanisms are diagnostic filters.  A source route that invokes a Regge tower
must give a survival projection, gauge representation, anomaly check,
mass-generation rule, and pole-width interpretation for every low positive
sector.  A Hodge or supersymmetric-quantum-mechanics route must produce a
normalized two-channel block whose square gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-kernel-target}
after electroweak projection.

The minimum acceptance criterion for the paper is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \mathcal D
  \Rightarrow
  \left[
  Q(J),\ (J_W,J_Z)=\left(\frac34,2\right),
  \right.\\
  \left.
  \Delta_{\rm match}=0,
  \mathcal F_{\rm sc}\ {\rm or\ auxiliary\ status}
  \right].
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:appendix-acceptance-criterion}
\end{equation}
Until Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-acceptance-criterion} is derived, the DeVries
relation remains a kinematical spectral clue.
