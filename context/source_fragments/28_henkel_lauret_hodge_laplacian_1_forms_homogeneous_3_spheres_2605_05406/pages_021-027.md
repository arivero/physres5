                                    6. Spectral inverse result
  In this section, we investigate the inverse spectral problem for the Hodge-Laplace operators
on homogeneous 3-spheres and the projective space SO(3). We focus on the spectrum of ∆1
acting on 1-forms. Note that due to the Hodge star isomorphism and the commutation with
the exterior derivative, the spectrum of ∆1 coincides with the spectrum of ∆2 and contains
the spectrum of the Laplacian on functions ∆0 (excluding zero). Thus, ∆1 contains the most
comprehensive spectral information.
  Furthermore, leveraging the explicit formula for the first eigenvalue established in Theorem
5.5, we prove that the spectrum of ∆1 determines the isometry class of the metric g(a,b,c)
unconditionally.

6.1. Spectral invariants. We show that for homogeneous 3-manifolds, several fundamental
geometric invariants are indeed spectrally determined.

Then the volume vol(M, g), the scalar curvature Scal, the norm of the full curvature tensor
∥R∥, and the norm of the Ricci tensor ∥ Ric ∥ are each spectral invariants of both the Hodge–
Laplacian and the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

6.2. Isospectrality results. Based on the spectral invariants derived above, we can now ad-
dress the inverse spectral problem. We first consider the case of non-positive scalar curvature,
where the heat invariants alone provide sufficient information.
Corollary 6.2. Within the class of homogeneous metrics on S 3 or SO(3) with non-positive
scalar curvature, the isometry class is uniquely determined by the spectrum of the Hodge-
Laplacian on p-forms for any single degree p ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}.
Proof. Kling and Schueth [KS22] investigated the first eigenvalue of the Dirac operator on
SU(2), SO(3) and they found that in the non-positive scalar curvature case, the following
determine the parameters a, b, c up to permutation uniquely:
     • The volume, vol,
     • the scalar curvature, Scal,
     • the value 5 Scal2 −8∥ Ric ∥2 − 7∥R∥2 .
However, by the last proposition, the geometric properties vol, ∥R∥2 , ∥ Ric ∥2 and Scal2 are
themselves already spectral invariants.                                                    □
   In the arbitrary case, Lin, Schmidt and Sutton (see [LSS21, Thm. 1.6]) proved that the first
three heat invariants do not determine the metric parameters. However, by incorporating the
information from the first eigenvalue, we obtain the following result.
                                     HODGE LAPLACIAN ON 1-FORMS                                            25

Theorem 6.3. The isometry class of (S 3 , g(a,b,c) ) or (SO(3), g(a,b,c) ) is uniquely determined by
the spectrum of the Hodge-Laplacian on p-forms for any single degree p ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}.
Proof. The spectrum of ∆1 determines the heat invariants a0 and a1 , which in turn determine
the volume and the scalar curvature Scal. Following the notation in [KS22], let x = a2 , y =
b2 , z = c2 and let σ1 , σ2 , σ3 denote their elementary symmetric polynomials, that is,
                          σ1 = x + y + z,      σ2 = xy + yz + zx,       σ3 = xyz.
The volume fixes the product σ3 .
  By the explicit formula for the first eigenvalue established in Theorem 5.5, λ1 provides a
third independent condition. We distinguish two cases:
    (1) The minimum is attained by the exact forms, i.e., λ1 = x + y + z. In this case, the
        spectrum determines σ1 = λ1 . The scalar curvature is given by (see [KS22, (17)])
                                                         σ2
                                           Scal = 8σ1 − 2 2 .
                                                         σ3
       Since σ1 , σ3 and Scal are known, this equation uniquely determines σ2 (as the positive
       solution of a quadratic equation). The metric parameters x, y, z are then the unique
       roots of the polynomial P (t) = t3 − σ1 t2 + σ2 t − σ3 , by Vieta’s formulas (see for example
       [We02]).
   (2) The minimum is attained by a coclosed form, e.g., λ1 = 4yz/x. Using σ3 = xyz, we
       can rewrite this as λ1 = 4σ3 /x2 . Thus, λ1 and σ3 uniquely determine one parameter,
       say x. With x known, the product of the remaining parameters is fixed by yz = σ3 /x.
       The scalar curvature equation then becomes a symmetric relation in y and z, which,
       together with their product, uniquely determines y and z.
  In both cases, the spectral data allows us to recover the parameters a, b, c up to permutation.
Thus, the isometry class is spectrally determined.                                                □
