\section{Dimensional interpolation target}
\label{app:dimensional-interpolation}

This appendix records a dimensional theorem target for the Higgs-sector
problem.  Its role is to organize the Kaluza--Klein, string, Higgs, and
top-quark questions through a common source package.

The source status has four levels.  Witten's seven-extra-dimensional
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) endpoint is source-backed.  The
five-extra-dimensional \(SU(3)\times U(1)_{\rm geom}\) endpoint is a geometric
endpoint target whose electromagnetic embedding remains to be derived.  The
\(D=10\) and colourless \(D=6\) middle lines are reconstruction labels for a
source sector that must supply \(\mathcal B\), \(\mathcal H_J\),
\(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_u\), \(P_J(u)\), \(\Lambda_J(u)\),
\(\widehat K_J\), a decoupling rule for extra channels, and
\(\mathcal R_{\rm pole}\).  The scalar branch and top-sector readings require
the same variable to control the gauge-Higgs ray, the reduced kernel, and the
relevant Yukawa or boundary datum.  The manuscript-facing source claims in this
appendix rest on Witten, CHM, the six-dimensional string sources, and local
\(G_2\) references.

\label{app:full-gauge-count}

quantum-number obstruction for that seven-dimensional compactification.

\subsection{Colourless electroweak count}
\label{app:colourless-count}

There is a reduced count in which colour is treated as a spectator sector.  The
electroweak part of the endpoint symmetry then changes as
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ccl}
D=7,\quad n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew}=3
&:&
SU(2)\times U(1),
\\[3pt]
D=6,\quad n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew}=2
&:&
\hbox{DeVries electroweak interpolation sector},
\\[3pt]
D=5,\quad n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew}=1
&:&
U(1)_{\rm geom}\to U(1)_{\rm em}\quad\hbox{after embedding theorem}.
\end{array}
\label{eq:colourless-dimensional-chain}
\end{equation}
This version gives the six-dimensional middle line a direct string-theory
source address.  Six-dimensional superstring vacua and dualities are a
standard part of the source landscape: type IIA compactified on K3 and the
heterotic string compactified on \(T^4\) provide a six-dimensional string-string
duality setting \cite{Strominger1995,Sen1995}; heterotic small instantons give
nonperturbative six-dimensional dynamics \cite{WittenSmallInstantons1995}; and
six-dimensional anomaly and string-universality constraints provide a modern
consistency framework \cite{KumarTaylor2009}.  These sources support the
existence and rigidity of six-dimensional string settings.  They provide a
constrained string-vacuum arena.  The colourless \(D=6\) line remains a
Schur-complement reconstruction label whose DeVries use requires the data in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:middle-dimensional-data}.

\subsection{Top-quark scale}
\label{app:top-quark-scale}

The dimensional interpolation also creates a top-sector question.  In the
Standard Model, fermion masses are generated by Yukawa couplings to the Higgs
vacuum expectation value,
\begin{equation}
  m_f=\frac{y_f v}{\sqrt2},
  \qquad
  g_{hff}=\frac{m_f}{v}.
  \label{eq:top-yukawa-sm-fact}
\end{equation}
The top quark has the largest direct fermionic coupling to the electroweak
order parameter.  This fact is visible in Higgs phenomenology, in electroweak
corrections with strong top-mass sensitivity, and in vacuum-stability analyses
\cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018,AlvarezGaumeVazquezMozo2023}.
Local \(G_2\) Higgs-bundle constructions also provide a source-theory template:
flow-tree data can engineer a top Yukawa coupling in an \(SU(5)\)-type local
model \cite{BraunEtAl2019}.
In the interval route, CHM supplies a separate top-sector lesson: a heavy top
pushes the third-generation localization and boundary mixing, while the
observed bottom coupling to the \(Z\) restricts the same data
\cite{CsakiHubiszMeade2005}.  A DeVries claim requires a map from that
localization or boundary datum to \(\mathcal Y_{\rm top}(u)\).

The DeVries question is sharper: can the six-dimensional interior sector
select the top quark as the fermion attached most directly to the order
parameter?  The source-theory target is

\end{equation}
with the top representation, localization or boundary data, anomaly and
global-form checks, and pole-scheme matching stated in the same basis as
\(\widehat K_J\).  The CHM interval route gives an active test package:
