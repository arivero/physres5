\section{Conclusion and analytical status}
\label{sec:status-tests}
% Status: derived/open. Summarizes established results, assumptions, and theorem targets.

The construction begins with an exact two-branch algebra.  The first
assumption \(A1\) fixes
\[
  Q(J)=\mu^2
  \begin{pmatrix}
  0&\sqrt J\\
  \sqrt J&-J
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  J=s(s+1),
\]
whose dimensionless eigenvalues obey \(x^2+Jx-J=0\).  The quotient
\[
  1-\frac{x_+(3/4)}{x_+(2)}
\]
is therefore a rigid consequence of \(A1\) once the ordered samples
\((3/4,2)\) are chosen.  The physical interpretation additionally requires
\(A2\) and \(A3\).

The placement hypothesis \(A2\) uses the complex-pole mass ratio
\[
  \sPole=1-\frac{M_{W,\rm pole}^2}{M_{Z,\rm pole}^2}.
\]
This choice gives the spectral language a low-energy meaning and separates the
proposal from \(\MSbar\) running angles, effective weak angles, Breit--Wigner
line-shape masses, and high-scale GUT boundary values.  The pole placement is
an assumption awaiting a dynamical pole condition.  Appendix~\ref{app:target-pole-placement}
states the corresponding theorem target.

The Standard Model gauge-Higgs sector supplies the projective datum
\[
  \frac{M_W^2}{M_Z^2}=\frac{g^2}{g^2+g'^2}
\]
and the broken-to-unbroken vacuum ray.  It also supplies two representation
data relevant to the proposed assignment: the Higgs/order-parameter doublet
with \(J_H=3/4\), and the adjoint/current channel with \(J_{\rm adj}=2\).
The assignment hypothesis \(A3\) is the ordered map
\[
  (J_W,J_Z)=(J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)
\]
from gauge-Higgs, boundary, endpoint, or compactification data.  The first
theorem target \(T1\) is a derivation of this map from the same source data
that define the two-channel operator.

The electroweak ray adds a separate admissibility condition.  A source route
must preserve one radial gauge-Higgs parameter, the photon null direction, and
the simultaneous collapse of the charged and neutral massive vector poles at
fixed projective direction.  Appendix~\ref{app:target-electroweak-ray} records
this rank-and-ray condition.  Coupling-space paths and custodial-breaking
shifts are separate deformation problems.

The pole convention, electroweak ray, theorem-target ledger, project-source
protocol, flavor boundary, and global-form filter serve as fixed
acceptance filters.  They define the scheme, deformation class, source
discipline, and completion tests that every route derivation has to satisfy.
The DeVries determinant remains the dynamical target.

The negative branch belongs to the same diagonalization as the positive branch.
Its scalar interpretation remains conditional on a gauge-invariant functional,
a normalization, and a scheme.  Candidate targets include a Higgs-potential
parameter, a scalar pole, a boundary modulus, a brane separation mode, or a
compactification deformation.  Any source theory that derives the
positive-branch determinant must also account for the partner eigenvalue.
The same condition governs any electromagnetic-coupling claim.  The
construction leaves \(\alpha\) as a Standard Model input: the scalar
normalization, the \(U(1)_{\rm em}\) generator and charge lattice, and the
matching pair \((Q_\alpha,\Delta_{\rm th})\) remain open source-theory data.
The electroweak content of the DeVries clue is the pole ratio \(\sPole\).

The second theorem target \(T2\) is the route-kernel derivation.  Endpoint,
interval, and \(G_2\) sources give addresses for a two-channel kernel.  In
common notation the required reduction is
\[
  K_J(\lambda)=
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda+\Sigma_{hh,J} & \Sigma_{ha,J}\\
  \Sigma_{ah,J} & \lambda+\Sigma_{aa,J}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \Sigma_{hh,J}=0,\quad
  \Sigma_{aa,J}=J,\quad
  \Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J .
\]
Open-string endpoint data, interval boundary conditions, and localized
\(G_2\) gauge sectors provide candidate arenas for such a kernel.  The
exact DeVries entries remain to be derived from one source theory with a
specified field basis and inner product.

The route analysis imposes three necessary consistency conditions.  Trace
statements stay inside the reduced matrices where they are derived until a
shared source operator identifies the trace spaces.  The tested
Wigner--Eckart/Clebsch parent routes obey their selection rules and miss the
parent data required by Eq.~\eqref{eq:route-common-target}.  An SO(32)-flavor
sector can constrain a completion after its projection, anomaly, hypercharge,
global-form, and electroweak-coupling obligations are supplied.

A source route that preserves a common Regge slope can place the DeVries roots
as intercepts in
\[
  M^2_{N_{\rm osc},j,\sigma}
  =
  \mu^2x_\sigma\!\big(j(j+1)\big)
  +\frac{N_{\rm osc}}{\alpha'}
  +\Delta^{\rm Regge}_{N_{\rm osc},j,\sigma}.
\]
The first higher positive sector is \(j=\tfrac32\), an algebraic assignment
ledger entry.  A particle interpretation requires \(\mathfrak R_{3/2}\):
survival projection, gauge representation, chirality or vectorlike status,
anomaly ledger, mass-generation rule, production and decay channels, widths,
and collider-source audit.  Appendix~\ref{app:target-regge-intercept-survival}
records this filter.

The interval route carries source-derived boundary machinery: variational
boundary conditions, modified inner products, vector Robin data, photon
zero-mode accounting, and scalar \(A_5/\pi_i\) equations.  The \(G_2\) route
carries localization and compact-completion machinery, with a scale-placement
burden before it reaches pole observables.  The electroweak superconnection
route packages odd Higgs data and even current data inside one gauge-Higgs
complex.  The unresolved entry equations in one reduced basis are
\[
  \Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J,
  \qquad
  \widehat\Sigma_{aa,J}^{\rm CHM}=J,
\]
with the source scale, reference subtraction, photon projection, and pole map
fixed from the same interval data.

The route-kernel ledger combines these two routes.  A boundary electroweak
superconnection on a CHM interval should give a hatted kernel
\[
  \widehat K_J^{\rm sc/int}
  =
  \Lambda_J^{-2}P_{\rm sc}^\dagger
  \left[
  K_T^{\rm DtN}+K_T^{\rm brane}
  +\langle\mathcal F_{\rm sc},\mathcal F_{\rm sc}\rangle_{\rm sc}
  -K_\gamma^{\rm ref}
  \right]P_{\rm sc}.
\]
The closure theorem targets are
\[
  P_W(\Phi_{\rm odd})=\frac34,\qquad
  P_Z(F_{\rm even}^{\gamma^\perp})=2,\qquad
  P_\gamma(F_{\rm even}^{\gamma})=0,
\]
extra-channel decoupling, the negative-branch scalar functional, and the
complex-pole remainder \(\Delta_{\rm O1/O10}\).

The resulting route tests are explicit.  Target I gives the pole-placement and
high-scale separation test.  Target II gives the ordered-assignment source
test.  Target III gives the common source-kernel entry tests.  Target IX gives
the brane-scaling admissibility diagnostic.  Target X gives the
Hodge/superconnection square-root and breaking-operator test.  The current W/Z
input audit is fixed in Sec.~\ref{sec:current-wz-audit}.  The remaining open
issue file is the negative-branch scalar functional.

The third theorem target \(T3\) is the scalar interpretation of the partner
branch.  The present paper establishes the algebraic clue, the pole-placement
hypothesis, and candidate arenas for the determinant.  A dynamical claim
for the DeVries quotient requires the two-channel determinant, the ordered
electroweak assignment, ray admissibility, the pole-matching theorem, and the
controlled scalar interpretation of the negative branch.  Global-form and
representation checks then constrain any compact completion.  Appendix~\ref{app:theorem-targets}
collects these requirements in theorem-target form.
