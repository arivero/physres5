\section{Theorem targets for a derivation}
\label{app:theorem-targets}
% Status: open. Collects theorem targets, source-backed constraints, and proof obligations.

This appendix records the analytical obligations in theorem form.  Each target
names its hypotheses, conclusion, current evidence, and failure mode.  A
complete version of the manuscript proves these statements, replaces them with
stronger statements, or demotes the dependent claims.

\subsection{Common format}
\label{app:theorem-format}

The targets share a common data structure.  Let \(\mathcal S\) be a source theory or effective description.  It may be a four-dimensional electroweak theory, a boundary effective action from an interval compactification, an open-string endpoint sector, or a localized \(G_2\) system.  Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be the two-dimensional light subspace that is meant to carry the order-parameter and current amplitudes.  The desired reduction has the form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_J
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal M_J
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  0 & \sqrt J\\
  \sqrt J & -J
  \end{pmatrix}
  \longrightarrow
  \{x_+(J),x_-(J)\}.
  \label{eq:appendix-reduction-chain}
\end{equation}
The reduction is meaningful after the normalization of \(\mathcal H_J\) and the scheme in which the resulting eigenvalues are read have been specified.  The pole-observable section fixes the electroweak comparison as a complex-pole statement, with Breit-Wigner/on-shell and running quantities treated as translated inputs \cite{MartinRobertson2025,CDFII2022}.  The electroweak section fixes the low-energy field-theory data that any assignment theorem must respect \cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018,AlvarezGaumeVazquezMozo2023}.  The string and Kaluza--Klein sections list candidate sources of the two-channel determinant \cite{TongString,Polchinski1994,CsakiHubiszMeade2005}.

For each target below, ``proof'' means an explicit derivation from a stated action, boundary condition, operator algebra, or compactification mode equation.  A plausibility argument may motivate the target, yet the manuscript's final physical claim must track the proven statement.

\subsection{Target 0: unified source-to-pole matching}
\label{app:target-unified-source-to-pole}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Let \(r\in\{\mathrm{end},\mathrm{int},G_2\}\) label an endpoint,
interval, or localized-singularity source description \(\mathcal S_r\).  The
source theory supplies a two-dimensional light subspace
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_{J,r}=\operatorname{span}\{h_{J,r},a_{J,r}\},
  \qquad
  \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_r,
  \qquad
  P_{J,r},
  \label{eq:target0-light-space}
\end{equation}
where the inner product and projection map \(P_{J,r}\) define the reduced
field basis.  Extra light channels are absent, integrated out, or
block-diagonalized by the same projection.  The route reduction has Schur form
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  =
  K^{\rm bdry}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  -
  V_{J,r}^{\dagger}(\lambda-L_{J,r})^{-1}V_{J,r}.
  \label{eq:target0-schur}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
In the reduced basis of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target0-light-space},
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda&-\sqrt J\\
  -\sqrt J&\lambda+J
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:target0-devries-kernel}
\end{equation}
The same field basis carries the electroweak sampling maps
\begin{equation}
  P_W:\ h_{J,r}\mapsto C_2(\mathbf 2)=\frac34,
  \qquad
  P_Z:\ a_{J,r}\mapsto C_2(\mathrm{adj})=2,
  \label{eq:target0-ew-projectors}
\end{equation}
and the source kernel contributes to the four-dimensional transverse
self-energy in the pole scheme,
\begin{equation}
  K^{\rm eff}_{J,r}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Pi^{(4)}_{T,V}(s;J,r)
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J,r)=0.
  \label{eq:target0-pole-chain}
\end{equation}
Thus one theorem supplies the source reduction, the reduced inner product, the
DeVries entries, the ordered W/Z sampling maps, and the pole-placement chain.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The endpoint, interval, and \(G_2\) sections supply source-backed addresses for
the three route types.  The interval route has the most explicit boundary
operator language, while the \(G_2\) route supplies localization language for
gauge and matter data.  The unified theorem is the common acceptance test
shared by the routes.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the source theory yields extra coupled light channels, a
route-dependent inner product, a different trace or mixing product, an
unordered electroweak sampling map, or a high-scale quantity whose matching to
complex poles changes the determinant.  Such an outcome classifies the source
as a neighboring spectral construction.

\subsection{Target I: pole placement}
\label{app:target-pole-placement}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume a source theory \(\mathcal S\) gives a two-branch spectral equation with roots \(x_\pm(J)\).  Assume the electroweak comparison is made with transverse gauge-boson complex poles,
\begin{equation}
  \det\Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V)=0,
  \qquad
  s_V=M_{V,\rm pole}^2-iM_{V,\rm pole}\Gamma_{V,\rm pole}.
  \label{eq:target-complex-pole}
\end{equation}
Assume the mass and width symbols are in the pole parameterization of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.  If an input uses the alternative
parameterization \(s_{\rm pole}=(M-i\Gamma/2)^2\), first translate it to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.  If W/Z inputs are quoted in a common
variable-width Breit--Wigner line-shape convention, convert
\begin{equation}
  M_{V,\rm pole}
  =
  \frac{M_{V,\rm BW}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_V^2}},
  \qquad
  \Gamma_{V,\rm pole}
  =
  \frac{\Gamma_{V,\rm BW}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_V^2}},
  \qquad
  \gamma_V=\frac{\Gamma_{V,\rm BW}}{M_{V,\rm BW}} .
  \label{eq:target-bw-width-map}
\end{equation}
This gives
\begin{equation}
  M_{V,\rm BW}^2=M_{V,\rm pole}^2+\Gamma_{V,\rm pole}^2 .
  \label{eq:target-bw-mass-map}
\end{equation}
Assume common experimental mass conventions are converted into the pole convention before forming
\begin{equation}
  \sPole=1-\frac{M_{W,\rm pole}^2}{M_{Z,\rm pole}^2}.
  \label{eq:target-spole}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The theorem target is a pole-spectrum identification:
\begin{equation}
  \sPole
  =
  1-\frac{x_+(J_H)}{x_+(J_{\rm adj})}
  \label{eq:target-pole-conclusion}
\end{equation}
up to stated radiative, threshold, and scheme translations.  The theorem must also explain why the assigned scale is the low-energy pole spectrum, with high-scale boundary placements handled by a separate derivation.
For any compactification, endpoint, interval, or local \(G_2\) source, the
scale-placement statement has the matching form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  K_J^{\rm bare}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Gamma_{\rm eff}[H,W,B]
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s)
  \big|_{s=s_{W,Z}} .
  \label{eq:target-pole-matching-chain}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is the source reduction.  The second arrow is four-dimensional
effective matching.  The third arrow is the dressed transverse pole condition.
The pole reading requires all three arrows in one scheme.  Target~0 packages
this requirement with the reduced field basis and electroweak sampling maps.
Equivalently, the source kernel must produce the relevant pole self-energy
term,
\begin{equation}
  K_J^{\rm bare}(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  \Pi_{T,V}^{(4)}(s;J),
  \qquad
  \Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J)=0,
  \label{eq:target-self-energy-chain}
\end{equation}
with the same pole parameterization and field basis used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-complex-pole}.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The manuscript already separates complex poles, Breit-Wigner/on-shell masses, \(\MSbar\) running angles, effective weak angles, GUT boundary values, and compactification eigenvalues.  Martin--Robertson supply the scheme language used in Sec.~\ref{sec:pole}.  The CDF-II result has descriptive displacement status here.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the derived clean value lives at a high-scale boundary, a running \(\MSbar\) angle, an effective leptonic angle, or a compactification eigenvalue with a different translation law.  In that case the pole-ratio reading becomes a motivating clue, and the manuscript relocates the physical statement to the derived scheme and scale.  A compactification-scale or unification-scale derivation would still be valuable; it would convert the DeVries quotient into a boundary datum whose relation to W/Z pole data is a separate running, threshold, and matching problem.

\subsection{Target II: electroweak assignment}
\label{app:target-electroweak-assignment}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Let \(H\) be the Standard Model Higgs doublet and let \(J_H=3/4\) be its \(SU(2)_L\) quadratic invariant.  Let \(J_{\rm adj}=2\) be the corresponding adjoint invariant for \(SU(2)_L\) currents.  The gauge-Higgs sector contains the usual mass terms
\begin{equation}
  M_W^2=\frac{g^2v^2}{4},
  \qquad
  M_Z^2=\frac{(g^2+g'^2)v^2}{4},
  \qquad
  M_\gamma^2=0,
  \label{eq:target-ew-masses}
\end{equation}
with the custodial structure and \(\rho=1\) tree-level relation discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ew-embedding}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The required theorem is the existence of a map from gauge-Higgs data to ordered samples,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D_{\rm EW}:
  (H,J^a_\mu,B_\mu)
  \longmapsto
  (J_W,J_Z)=(J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2),
  \label{eq:target-ew-map}
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}
  \frac{M_W^2}{M_Z^2}
  =
  \frac{x_+(J_H)}{x_+(J_{\rm adj})}.
  \label{eq:target-ew-conclusion}
\end{equation}
The derivation must say why the charged comparison samples the order-parameter invariant while the neutral comparison samples the adjoint/current invariant.  The allowed mathematical channels for the charged sample are: a Higgs-representation contribution inside the gauge-Higgs mass map, a scalar contribution to the W transverse self-energy, or a source/boundary label coupled to the Higgsing field.  Assigning \(C_2(\mathbf 2)\) to the Higgs field and \(C_2(\mathrm{adj})\) to the gauge field leaves the pole-ratio comparison descriptive until the ordered W/Z sampling rule is derived.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
The Higgs doublet and adjoint invariants are fixed representation-theoretic facts.  The electroweak mass matrix, photon null direction, custodial relation, and projective reading of \(g:g'\) are standard low-energy data \cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018,AlvarezGaumeVazquezMozo2023}.  The manuscript has converted the assignment into a theorem target.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the assignment uses observed vector spin or an arbitrary post-selection of \(J\) values.  A successful derivation must arise from the gauge-Higgs variables, a boundary operator coupled to them, or a compactification mechanism whose low-energy reduction yields the ordered pair.

\subsection{Target III: two-channel boundary determinant}
\label{app:target-boundary-determinant}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Let \(h_J\) be an order-parameter amplitude and \(a_J\) an adjoint/current amplitude in a light boundary subspace
\[
  \mathcal H_J=\operatorname{span}\{h_J,a_J\}
\]
with a stated inner product, projection map from the source Hilbert space, and
normalization rule.  Extra channels are integrated out or block-diagonalized
before the two-by-two kernel is compared with the DeVries target.  Let the
normalized quadratic kernel be
\begin{equation}
  K_J(\lambda)=
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda+\Sigma_{hh,J} & \Sigma_{ha,J}\\
  \Sigma_{ah,J} & \lambda+\Sigma_{aa,J}
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:target-boundary-kernel}
\end{equation}
The source may be an interval theory with brane-localized terms, an open-string endpoint sector, or a localized singularity construction.  Boundary mass and kinetic terms can alter eigenvalue conditions in extra-dimensional models \cite{CsakiHubiszMeade2005}.  Open-string endpoints and D-brane gauge sectors supply a separate endpoint language \cite{TongString,Polchinski1994}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
There is a controlled field basis, inner product, projection, and
normalization in which
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{hh,J}=0,\qquad
  \Sigma_{aa,J}=J,\qquad
  \Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J,
  \label{eq:target-boundary-self-energies}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \det K_J(\lambda)=\lambda^2+J\lambda-J.
  \label{eq:target-boundary-conclusion}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the physical derivation fixes the two named entries
\begin{equation}
  \tau_J=J,\qquad \kappa_J^2=J.
  \label{eq:target-kappa-tau}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
Sec.~\ref{sec:toy-boundary-operator} gives the target action and the required entries.  The literature sources support the existence of boundary-condition and endpoint mechanisms, while the exact DeVries entries remain conjectural.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if boundary variation gives a different trace, a different off-diagonal product, or an additional channel that changes the determinant class.  The result in that case would be a larger family of secular relations containing the DeVries matrix as a limiting or approximate case.

\subsection{Target IV: negative branch as scalar data}
\label{app:target-negative-branch}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume Targets I--III identify a physical two-channel matrix whose eigenvalues are \(x_\pm(J)\).  Let \(u_\pm(J)\) be normalized eigenvectors.  The negative branch is then part of the same diagonalization as the positive branch:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal M_J u_\pm(J)=x_\pm(J)u_\pm(J).
  \label{eq:target-eigenvectors}
\end{equation}
The scalar side of the electroweak theory contains the gauge-invariant radial datum, the Higgs pole, and the parameters of the symmetry-breaking potential \cite{Logan2022,Dawson2018}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
There exists a gauge-invariant scalar functional \(\mathcal F_{\rm sc}\) such that
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_{\rm sc}\big(H^\dagger H,\;D_\mu H,\;\text{boundary data}\big)
  =
  C\,|x_-(J_\star)|
  \label{eq:target-scalar-functional}
\end{equation}
for a stated \(J_\star\), normalization \(C\), and scheme.  Candidate readings include a Higgs-potential parameter, the radial vacuum scale, a boundary modulus, or a brane-separation mode.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
Sec.~\ref{sec:negative} records the scalar target table and the eigenvector interpretation.  The negative branch is treated as an analytical partner of the pole clue, with its Higgs/order-parameter meaning open.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the negative branch has purely auxiliary meaning in the derived source theory.  The positive-branch clue can still motivate the determinant problem, while the scalar claims must then be removed from the physical conclusion.

\subsection{Target V: global-form and localization compatibility}
\label{app:target-global-form}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume a string, brane, Kaluza--Klein, or \(G_2\) source derives Targets II--IV.  The low-energy gauge group has global form
\begin{equation}
  \GSM=
  \frac{SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)}{\Gamma},
  \qquad
  \Gamma\subseteq \mathbb Z_6.
  \label{eq:target-global-group}
\end{equation}
Line operators and global identifications provide tests of the Standard Model gauge group beyond the Lie algebra \cite{TongLineOperators2017,HsinGomis2024}.  Singular \(G_2\) spaces can localize gauge and chiral matter sectors in M-theory constructions \cite{WittenG2Anomaly2001,AcharyaWitten2001,AcharyaGukov2004,BraunEtAl2019}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The derived operator \(\mathcal M_J\) is compatible with the allowed line operators, representation assignments, anomaly constraints, and localized matter data of the chosen completion.  A successful completion must specify whether flavor labels are endpoint data, compactification data, representation-sector data, or a combination constrained by the source geometry.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
Secs.~\ref{sec:flavour} and \ref{sec:global-form} state the current boundary conditions: the SO(32)-flavor idea is a representation-sector clue, and global-form data are completion constraints.  The manuscript avoids identifying generations with compactification topology until a representation map is derived.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the determinant arises in a gauge or flavor sector incompatible with Standard Model line operators, anomaly conditions, or observed representations.  A failure here would be decisive for a proposed completion even if the local two-channel determinant is mathematically correct.

\subsection{Target VI: dimensional interpolation}
\label{app:target-dimensional-interpolation}

\paragraph*{Hypotheses.}
Assume the electroweak order parameter supplies an interpolation parameter
\(t\), represented in a source theory by compact, boundary, brane, or singular
data.  The source-backed full-gauge endpoint is
\begin{equation}
  (D,n_{\rm KK})=(11,7):
  SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1).
  \label{eq:target-full-dimensional-source-endpoint}
\end{equation}
The five-extra-dimensional endpoint is a reconstruction target until its
electroweak embedding identifies the surviving \(U(1)\) with
\(U(1)_{\rm em}\):
\begin{equation}
  (D,n_{\rm KK})=(9,5):
  SU(3)\times U(1)_{\rm em}.
  \label{eq:target-full-dimensional-endpoints}
\end{equation}
The colourless electroweak dictionary is
\begin{equation}
  (D,n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew})=(7,3):
  SU(2)\times U(1),
  \qquad
  (D,n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew})=(5,1):
  U(1)_{\rm em}.
  \label{eq:target-colourless-dimensional-endpoints}
\end{equation}
At the middle point \(t=t_\star\), assume a light subspace
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_J(t_\star)=\operatorname{span}\{h_J,a_J\}
  \label{eq:target-dimensional-light-space}
\end{equation}
with \(h_J\) an order-parameter channel and \(a_J\) an adjoint/current
channel.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
The middle-dimensional sector defines a source operator
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal O_J(t_\star)\big|_{\mathcal H_J}
  \longrightarrow
  K_J(t_\star,\lambda)
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  \lambda&-\sqrt J\\
  -\sqrt J&\lambda+J
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:target-dimensional-kernel}
\end{equation}
and, through Target~\ref{app:target-electroweak-assignment}, the same sector
yields the ordered electroweak samples
\begin{equation}
  (J_W,J_Z)=(J_H,J_{\rm adj})=\left(\frac34,2\right).
  \label{eq:target-dimensional-assignment}
\end{equation}
The full-gauge middle line is then \((D,n_{\rm KK})=(10,6)\).  The
colourless middle line is \((D,n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew})=(6,2)\).  The theorem
also supplies the pole-matching chain in Eq.~\eqref{eq:target-pole-matching-chain}.

\paragraph*{Current evidence.}
Witten's Kaluza--Klein analysis supplies the source trail for the
seven-extra-dimensional \(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) endpoint and the
associated fermion quantum-number obstruction \cite{WittenKK1981}.  The same
paper contains a five-sphere \(SU(3)\times U(1)\) symmetry address.  The
six-dimensional string sources support a landscape of six-dimensional string
settings and dualities \cite{Strominger1995,Sen1995,WittenSmallInstantons1995,KumarTaylor2009}.
Appendix~\ref{app:dimensional-interpolation} converts these facts into a
source-status table and a middle-line reconstruction target.

\paragraph*{Failure mode.}
The target fails if the middle-dimensional sector produces extra light
channels, a kernel outside the DeVries determinant class, a high-scale datum
with a different matching law, or an ordered assignment other than
\((J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)\).  Such an outcome would classify the dimensional
idea as an organizing analogy for a different source-theory reduction.

\subsection{Claim hierarchy and acceptance criteria}
\label{app:claim-hierarchy}

The theorem targets separate four kinds of statements:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}}
\toprule
Level & Manuscript content & Required evidence\\
\midrule
Derived & algebra of \(Q(J)\), roots, branch identities, trace and determinant form & equation-level derivation in Sec.~\ref{sec:casimir}\\
Source-supported & electroweak mass matrix, pole/running scheme distinctions, endpoint/brane, KK, and \(G_2\) background & cited local source fragments and references\\
Conjectural & pole placement of the DeVries quotient, negative-branch scalar reading, route-specific kernel entries & theorem target with assumptions and failure mode\\
Open & ordered \(J\)-assignment, full determinant derivation, scalar functional, global-form compatibility of a compact completion & proof from a stated action, boundary condition, endpoint algebra, or localized operator\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A minimal derivation has the chain
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal S
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_J
  \longrightarrow
  K_J(\lambda)
  \longrightarrow
  (J_H,J_{\rm adj})
  \longrightarrow
  (\sPole,\mathcal F_{\rm sc}).
  \label{eq:appendix-minimum-viable-derivation}
\end{equation}
The source theory \(\mathcal S\) may be a boundary effective action, an
endpoint sector, or a localized \(G_2\) system.  The light subspace
\(\mathcal H_J\) must contain an order-parameter channel and an adjoint/current
channel.  The kernel \(K_J(\lambda)\) must reduce to the DeVries determinant in
a stated normalization.  The ordered pair \((J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)\) must
arise from gauge-Higgs or source-theory data.  The final arrow must attach the
positive branch to the pole observable and the negative branch to a defined
scalar functional.

The common acceptance criterion is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \det K_J(\lambda)&=\lambda^2+J\lambda-J,\\
  (J_W,J_Z)&=(3/4,2),\\
  u_-(J)&\mapsto\mathcal F_{\rm sc},\\
  K_J^{\rm bare}
  &\to\Pi^{(4)}_{T,V}(s;J)
  \to\Delta^{-1}_{V,T}(s_V;J)=0 .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:appendix-acceptance-criterion}
\end{equation}
Partial results still sharpen the construction when they prove one arrow in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-minimum-viable-derivation}.  An interval model may
derive the determinant class, an endpoint model may derive the ordered
representation pair, and a \(G_2\) model may supply a localization mechanism.
Each result changes the status of a corresponding theorem target.

\subsection{Derivation checklist}
\label{app:derivation-checklist}

The theorem targets lead to the following checklist for future revisions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item State the source theory \(\mathcal S\) and the light subspace \(\mathcal H_J\).
\item Specify the normalization that makes \(\Sigma_{hh,J}=0\), \(\Sigma_{aa,J}=J\), and \(\Sigma_{ha,J}\Sigma_{ah,J}=J\).
\item Derive the ordered assignment \((J_H,J_{\rm adj})=(3/4,2)\) from low-energy gauge-Higgs or boundary data.
\item Give the pole-scheme translation for every quoted W/Z mass input.
\item Identify the negative branch as scalar data, auxiliary data, or a derived sector with another physical role.
\item Check global-form, anomaly, and representation compatibility.
\end{enumerate}
This checklist is the manuscript's current definition of a complete derivation.
