# Referee report round 3

## Central claim

The draft now presents the DeVries/Rivero secular equation as a pole-spectrum clue whose physical value depends on deriving the same two-branch determinant from electroweak, string, or Kaluza--Klein data.

## Strengths

- The pole observable is defined at the level of scheme categories: complex pole, Breit-Wigner/on-shell inputs, running weak angle, GUT boundary value, and compactification eigenvalue.
- The electroweak section states the assignment problem in theorem form, with the Higgs doublet and adjoint/current channels separated.
- The toy endpoint-boundary operator gives named entries \(\kappa_J\) and \(\tau_J\), making the determinant target reviewable.
- The negative branch has a scalar-side target table and an eigenvector reading.
- The manuscript now states source-backed background and conjectural targets separately in most sections.

## Main criticisms

- The paper still lacks a compact appendix collecting every theorem target, assumption, consequence, and required proof.
- The toy operator gains force once the manuscript states what field-theory or worldsheet assumptions would determine \(\kappa_J^2=J\) and \(\tau_J=J\).
- The historical string-theory positioning should be deeper. The claim that this revives the old string particle-spectrum ambition needs a source-audited line through dual models, endpoint data, D-branes, and Kaluza--Klein spectra.
- The source matrix needs additional page-level anchors for custodial symmetry, complex poles, boundary conditions, and \(G_2\) localization.
- The 60-page PRD target requires more conceptual derivation, diagrams, and appendices.
