                      5. Proof of the First Eigenvalue Conjecture
   In this section, we provide a rigorous proof of the First Eigenvalue Conjecture (Conjecture
3.7). As outlined in the introduction, the core analytical strategy for this proof—transforming
the Curl operator to the round metric to bound the coexact spectrum—was autonomously
discovered by ChatGPT 5.4 Pro. It took the model 100 minutes of non-stop computational
reasoning to derive this mathematical result. We note that this conclusion is based on a
computationally intensive, highly restricted tier (USD 200/month, limited to 15 queries), which
exceeds the capabilities of standard, freely available AI tools by far. In what follows, we
detail the complete mathematical argument, rewritten and carefully embedded in the relevant
literature.

5.1. The curl operator and the Hodge-Laplacian. On a Riemannian manifold (M, g) of
dimension n, the composition ∗g d defines an endomorphism on p-forms if and only if n = 2p+1.
In our specific setting of 3-manifolds (n = 3), this holds for 1-forms (p = 1). We thus define
the Curl operator on Ω1 (M ) as Curlg := ∗g d. The connection between the spectral properties
of Curlg and the Hodge Laplacian ∆1 is established in the literature.
Proposition 5.1 ([Bä19, CV26]). Let (M, g) be a closed, oriented Riemannian 3-manifold.
     (1) The operator Curlg = ∗g d acting on the space of coexact 1-forms δg Ω2 (M ) is a self-
         adjoint operator with discrete spectrum accumulating at ±∞, and 0 is not an eigenvalue
         [CV26, Thm. 2.1].
     (2) On the space of coexact 1-forms, the Hodge Laplacian satisfies ∆1 = Curl2g [Bä19,
         Lem. 2.3].
     (3) Consequently, a coexact 1-form α is an eigenform of ∆1 with eigenvalue λ if and only
         if α is a linear combination of eigenforms of Curlg whose eigenvalues µ satisfy µ2 = λ
         [Bä19, Lem. 2.3].
   The spectrum of the Curl operator has been computed on Berger 3-spheres (b = c) by Gibbons
[Gi80, Eq. (5.11)] in a physics context and analyzed by Capoferri and Vassiliev [CV26, Thm.
D.1]. The authors cannot be sure whether this was the inspiration for the AI, as the model did
not provide citations and its internal ‘Chain of Thought’ log was not insightful.
   To bound the non-zero eigenvalues of ∆1 on coexact 1-forms from below, it is strictly sufficient
to bound the absolute value of the eigenvalues of Curlg . Write g0 = g(1,1,1) for the round
metric on SU(2) or SO(3) with constant sectional curvature 1. Recall that the left-invariant
1-forms dual to the standard basis (E1 , E2 , E3 ) of su(2) are denoted by E1∗ , E2∗ , E3∗ , where
dE1∗ = −2E2∗ ∧ E3∗ (and cyclically). For the metric g = g(a,b,c) , the basis ( a1 E1∗ , 1b E2∗ , 1c E3∗ ) is
orthonormal, and the Riemannian volume form is µg = abc   1
