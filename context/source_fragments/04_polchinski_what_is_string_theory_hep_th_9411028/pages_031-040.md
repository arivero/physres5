1.8    Riemann Surfaces

Thus far we have focussed on local properties, without regard to the global structure or
boundary conditions. For string theory we will be interested in conformal field theories on
closed manifolds. The appropriate manifold for a two-dimensional CFT to live on is a two-
dimensional complex manifold, a Riemann surface. One can imagine this as being built up
from patches, patch i having a coordinate z (i) which runs over some portion of the complex
plane. If patches i and j overlap, there is a relation between the coordinates,

                                        z (j) = fij (z (i) )                            (1.8.1)

with fij an analytic function. Two Riemann surfaces are equivalent if there is a mapping
between them such that the coordinates on one are analytic functions of the coordinates on
the other. This is entirely parallel to the definition of a differentiable manifold, but it has

                                                34
more structure—the manifold comes with a local notion of analyticity. Since in a CFT each
field has a specific transformation law under analytic changes of coordinates, the transition
function (1.8.1) is just the information needed to extend the field from patch to patch.

    Any closed oriented oriented two-dimensional surface can be obtained by adding h handles

places, but my lectures have been most influenced by the approach in refs [37]-[40]. Let z (1)

different Riemann surfaces. For complex q, cut out the circles |z (1) |, |z (2) | < (1 − ǫ)|q|1/2 and

minus 3 from an overcounting due to the Möbius group, leaving 3h − 3 which is the correct
number of complex moduli. An index theorem states that the number of complex moduli
minus the number of conformal Killing transformations is 3h − 3, as we indeed have in each
case.
