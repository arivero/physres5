# Referee B normalization round 2

## Central claim

The manuscript's string/KK/\(G_2\) program is a theorem-target framework:
derive the DeVries quadratic as a normalized two-channel kernel, assign
\(J_H=3/4\) and \(J_{\rm adj}=2\) to the W/Z pole ratio, and treat the
negative branch as scalar/order-parameter data. Appendix E extends this
into the conjectural O10 dimensional interpolation \(D=11\to10\to9\),
plus the colourless \(D=7\to6\to5\) version.

## Strongest source-supported result

The strongest part is the source-controlled route comparison in sections
VI.C, VI.D, and VI.G. The interval section uses boundary variational data
and brane-localized terms as mechanisms capable of producing secular
determinants. The \(G_2\) section gives a credible local dictionary from
singular gauge loci, localized matter, Higgs-bundle variables, and
deformation modes to a possible local operator. The shared kernel target is
cleanly stated in Sec. VI.G, and the acceptance conditions are explicit in
Appendix D.

## Weakest inference

The O10 interpolation in `E_dimensional_interpolation.tex` is the weakest
inference. The endpoints have a source trail, especially Witten's
seven-extra-dimensional Standard Model gauge-group discussion, while the
middle entries \(D=10,n_{\rm KK}=6\) and \(D=6,n_{\rm KK}^{\rm ew}=2\)
remain labels for an unknown object. The six-dimensional string sources
cited in Appendix E support the existence of six-dimensional string
settings. They leave the DeVries determinant, the ordered \(J\)-assignment,
and pole placement as open derivation targets. The
\(SU(3)\times U(1)_{\rm em}\) endpoint needs a sharper source separation
from Witten's five-sphere \(SU(3)\times U(1)\) construction.

## Hidden assumptions

The dimensional count assumes that electroweak Higgsing can be represented
as loss of one KK internal dimension in the full-gauge chain and in the
colourless chain. The manuscript also assumes that treating colour as a
spectator preserves the relevant compactification logic. The proposed
evasion of Witten's seven-dimensional fermion obstruction assumes the
physical DeVries point is genuinely six-dimensional and inherits the needed
chiral structure. The kernel program assumes a protected two-state light
subspace, absence or decoupling of extra light channels, a normalization
with \(\Sigma_{hh}=0\), and route-independent entries \(\Sigma_{aa}=J\),
\(\Sigma_{ha}\Sigma_{ah}=J\). O8 remains open because compactification or
local \(G_2\) kernels naturally live at boundary or high scales, while the
claimed comparison is a low-energy complex-pole statement.
