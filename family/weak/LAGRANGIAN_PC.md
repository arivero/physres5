# Wigner–Eckart computation of the Casimir-locked off-diagonal

Status: explicit sympy calculation, two cases. **Honest headline: the
Wigner–Eckart conjecture of PAPER.md §6(a) does NOT close the
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ derivation gap.** Both candidate parent reps give
matrix elements that do not equal $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$. The doublet case
($R=2$) is ruled out by an SU(2) selection rule; the triplet case
($R=3$) gives a non-zero broken-generator matrix element, but it is the
quadratic Casimir of the *parent* group restricted to the singlet, not
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ of the daughter.

---

## 1. Statement of the question

The Casimir-locked seed of PAPER.md Eq.~(1) is
$$
M_R^2 \;=\; m_0^2 \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sqrt{C_2(R)} \\ \sqrt{C_2(R)} & -C_2(R) \end{pmatrix},
\qquad C_2(R)=j(j+1) \text{ with } j=(\dim R-1)/2,
$$
and the LAGRANGIAN.md attempt (§5) establishes that no single
adjoint-spurion mixing $\epsilon^a\,\Psi^\dagger T^a\chi$ produces the
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ off-diagonal — such a contraction saturates at
$j(R)<\sqrt{C_2(R)}=\sqrt{j(j+1)}$.

PAPER.md §6(a) and LAGRANGIAN.md §6 conjecture the resolution: embed
$(\Psi,\chi)$ as the singlet $\oplus$ rep-$R$ sub-blocks of a single
parent multiplet $R'$ of a parent group $G\supset \mathrm{SU}(2)_W$,
and read the off-diagonal as a Wigner–Eckart matrix element of $T^a$
between the singlet and rep-$R$ sub-blocks.

We test this conjecture for the two cases that arise in the paper:
$R=2$ (doublet) embedded in $\mathrm{SU}(3)\supset\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and
$R=3$ (triplet) embedded in $\mathrm{SO}(4)=\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times
\mathrm{SU}(2)_R\supset\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$. All matrix algebra is performed
symbolically with sympy; values quoted below are exact rationals or
surds.

---

## 2. Case A: SU(3) fundamental $\mathbf 3\to \mathbf 2\oplus\mathbf 1$

**Setup.** $G=\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $R'=$ fundamental $\mathbf 3$. The
$\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)$ acts on the first two components.
The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ generators in $R'$ are
$$
T^a_{\mathbf 3} \;=\; \frac12\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^a & 0\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a=1,2,3.
$$
Basis: $|e_1\rangle=(1,0,0)^T$ (doublet, $m=+\tfrac12$),
$|e_2\rangle=(0,1,0)^T$ (doublet, $m=-\tfrac12$),
$|e_3\rangle=(0,0,1)^T$ (singlet).

**Direct sympy computation** (verified):
$$
T^a_{\mathbf 3}\,|e_3\rangle \;=\; 0 \quad \text{for } a=1,2,3.
$$
The $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ generator is **block-diagonal** in the
$\mathbf 2\oplus \mathbf 1$ branching: it acts as $\sigma^a/2$ on the
doublet block and as 0 on the singlet. Hence
$$
\sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{m=\pm 1/2} \bigl|\langle e_3 | T^a_{\mathbf 3} | e_m\rangle\bigr|^2 \;=\; 0.
$$
**Result: matrix element is identically zero, not $\sqrt{C_2(2)}=\sqrt{3}/2$.**

**Why (Wigner–Eckart selection rule).** A vector ($k=1$) tensor operator
between $j_1=0$ and $j_2=1/2$ requires the triangle $|k-j_2|\le j_1\le
k+j_2$, i.e.\ $1/2\le 0$, which is false. Equivalently,
$1\otimes 1/2 = 1/2 \oplus 3/2$ contains no singlet — there is no
Clebsch–Gordan coefficient $\langle 0\,0|\,1/2\,m;1\,q\rangle$ available.

**Supplementary: SU(3) broken generators.** The Gell-Mann matrices
$\lambda_{4,5,6,7}/2$ connect the singlet sub-block to the doublet
sub-block. Direct sympy gives
$$
\sum_{i\in\{4,5,6,7\}}\sum_{m=\pm 1/2}\bigl|\langle e_3|\tfrac{\lambda_i}{2}|e_m\rangle\bigr|^2 \;=\; 4\times(1/2)^2 \;=\; 1.
$$
This is **the reduced matrix element of the broken-generator quartet
of $\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{SU}(2)$**, not $\sqrt{C_2(2)}=\sqrt{3}/2$. The
broken generators are not $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ generators, so they cannot
supply the "$T^a$" of the seed in any case.

**Verdict Case A.** No matrix element of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ generator
in $\mathbf 3$ of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ connects singlet to doublet. The
matrix element is **zero**, by an SU(2) Wigner–Eckart selection rule
that does not depend on the parent group. **The doublet off-diagonal of
the seed cannot be generated by Wigner–Eckart of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$
generator in any parent rep**, because $1\otimes 1/2$ has no singlet.

## 3. Case B: SO(4) bidoublet $(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 2)\to \mathbf 1\oplus \mathbf 3$

**Setup.** $G=\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$, $R'=(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 2)$
bidoublet (= 4-dim vector of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$). Diagonal subgroup
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_V\subset \mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ has
generators $T_V^a = T_L^a + T_R^a$. Branching:
$(\mathbf 2,\mathbf 2) = \mathbf 1\oplus \mathbf 3$ of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$
(antisymmetric $\oplus$ symmetric combinations).

Basis: tensor product $|\epsilon_L,\epsilon_R\rangle$ for
$\epsilon_{L,R}\in\{+,-\}$. CG decomposition:
$$
|0,0\rangle_V \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(|+,-\rangle - |-,+\rangle), \quad
|1,m\rangle_V \;=\; \{|+,+\rangle,\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(|+,-\rangle + |-,+\rangle),\,|-,-\rangle\}.
$$

### 3.1 Unbroken diagonal generator $T_V^a$

By construction, $T_V^a |0,0\rangle = 0$ (the singlet is annihilated by
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$ generators). Direct sympy verification:
$$
T_V^a |0,0\rangle = 0 \quad \forall a, \qquad
\sum_{a,m}\bigl|\langle 0,0|T_V^a|1,m\rangle\bigr|^2 = 0.
$$
**Result: matrix element of the unbroken $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$ generator is
identically zero.** This is the universal statement: the SU(2)
generator kills its own singlet.

### 3.2 Broken (axial) generator $T_A^a = T_L^a - T_R^a$

The axial combination is the SU(2)$_V$-rank-1 (triplet) operator built
from the broken generators. By Wigner–Eckart it CAN connect singlet to
triplet (triangle $|1-1|\le 0\le 1+1$ is satisfied).

Direct sympy:
$$
\sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{m=-1}^{+1}\bigl|\langle 0,0|T_A^a|1,m\rangle\bigr|^2 \;=\; 3.
$$
Equivalently $\langle 0,0|T_A^a T_A^a|0,0\rangle = 3$, since $T_A$ maps
the singlet entirely into the triplet (only $j'=1$ is reachable from
$j=0$ by a rank-1 tensor).

**Comparison.** $\sqrt{C_2(R=3)} = \sqrt 2 \approx 1.4142$, so the
target is $|\mathrm{ME}|^2 = 2$. We get **3, not 2**. Ratio $3/2$.

### 3.3 General SO(4) parent rep $(j,j)$

A simple algebraic identity:
$T_A^2 = (T_L-T_R)^2 = T_L^2 + T_R^2 - 2T_L\cdot T_R$ and
$T_V^2 = T_L^2 + T_R^2 + 2T_L\cdot T_R$, so
$T_A^2 = 2T_L^2 + 2T_R^2 - T_V^2$.

On the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_V$ singlet sub-block of any $(j_L,j_R)$ parent
rep (which exists only for $j_L = j_R = j$), $T_L^2 = T_R^2 = j(j+1)$
and $T_V^2|s\rangle = 0$, so
$$
\langle s|T_A^2|s\rangle \;=\; 4\,j(j+1) \;=\; 4\,C_2(j) \text{ of each factor}.
$$
By Wigner–Eckart, this entire quantity is the squared reduced matrix
element from singlet to *some* triplet sub-block (the unique $j'=1$
reachable from $j_1=0$ by a rank-1 tensor). Sympy confirms for
$(j_L,j_R) = (1/2,1/2)$ this is 3, and for $(1,1)$ it is 8:

| Parent $(j_L,j_R)$ | $\dim$ | $\langle s|T_A^2|s\rangle = 4j(j+1)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $(1/2,1/2)$ bidoublet | 4 | **3** |
| $(1,1)$           | 9  | 8 |
| $(3/2,3/2)$       | 16 | 15 |
| $(2,2)$           | 25 | 24 |

**Required to match $C_2(R=3)=2$:** $4j(j+1) = 2$, i.e.\ $j(j+1) = 1/2$.
This has **no allowed value** (integer or half-integer). The closest
is the bidoublet, off by a factor $3/2$.

### 3.4 Verdict Case B

The Wigner–Eckart matrix element of the broken (axial) generator in the
natural SO(4) bidoublet parent rep gives squared modulus $3$, not the
required $C_2(R=3)=2$. For no SO(4) $(j,j)$ parent rep does the sum
equal $C_2(R=3)$. The matrix element computed by Wigner–Eckart is
controlled by the Casimir of the *parent* factor groups, not by
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ of the daughter SU(2)$_V$ rep.

## 4. Synthesis: Wigner–Eckart does NOT derive $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$

**Direct answer.** In neither case does the Wigner–Eckart matrix
element of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ generator $T^a_{R'}$ between singlet
and rep-$R$ sub-blocks equal $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$.

For $R=2$ the matrix element vanishes by an SU(2) selection rule that
applies to ANY parent group and ANY embedding (the doublet $j=1/2$
cannot be reached from the singlet $j=0$ by a rank-1 tensor). For $R=3$
the matrix element of the unbroken generator vanishes (T_V kills its
own singlet), and the matrix element of the broken (axial) generator is
$4j(j+1)$ of the parent factor, which never equals $C_2(R=3)=2$.

**What the matrix element *is*.** For the natural SO(4) bidoublet:
$\sum_{a,m}|\langle 0|T_A^a|1,m\rangle|^2 = 3 = $ the broken-generator
content of the singlet, $= 2\,C_2(j_L) + 2\,C_2(j_R)$. By
Wigner–Eckart this is the squared reduced matrix element of the
*axial* rank-1 tensor between singlet and triplet, which has no
algebraic connection to $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ of the unbroken SU(2)$_V$.

**Why the conjecture fails.** The seed off-diagonal $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ is a
*square root* of the diagonal Casimir $C_2(R)$ of the rep-$R$
sub-block. Wigner–Eckart matrix elements of generators are
*reduced matrix elements* — quantities determined by the parent group
representation theory, not by the Casimir of any daughter rep. The
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ scaling is a relation BETWEEN diagonal and off-diagonal
seed entries, not a fact about any single matrix element.

**What additional structure would close the gap?** Three readings:

1. **A bilinear relation at the parent level**, not a single matrix
   element. The seed entries $M^2_{22} = -C_2(R)m_0^2$ and $|M^2_{12}|^2
   = C_2(R)m_0^4$ together imply $|M^2_{12}|^2 = -M^2_{22}\cdot m_0^2$.
   This is a constraint on TWO different operators (the mass term and
   the mixing term) sharing the same UV coefficient $m_0^2$. It is
   NOT a single Clebsch coefficient. The right reading is therefore an
   *engineered tuning* between two Wilson coefficients of the EFT, not
   a derivation from group theory.

2. **A non-renormalizable square-root operator** $\,S\sqrt{\Phi^\dagger
   T^a T^a\Phi}\,$ in the EFT below confinement (LAGRANGIAN.md §3,
   $\mathcal{L}_{\rm best}$). This operator is analytic in the broken
   phase (where $|\Phi|>0$) but not in $\Phi$, so it is not a Wilson
   coefficient of a UV-completed renormalizable Lagrangian. It is the
   right *shape* but cannot be derived as a Wigner–Eckart Clebsch.

3. **A rescaling of fields**. In the basis $\tilde s = s$,
   $\tilde\phi = \phi/\sqrt{C_2(R)}$, the off-diagonal becomes
   $C_2(R)$ and the matrix is $m_0^2 C_2(R)\,[[0,1],[1,-1]]$. The
   shape is then manifestly proportional to $C_2(R)$, and the
   $\sqrt{C_2}$ is a kinetic-normalization artifact of the
   original basis. But this rescaling is rep-dependent: the field
   redefinition $\tilde\phi_R = \phi_R/\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ has a different
   coefficient for $R=2$ and $R=3$. The shared seed scale $m_0$ is
   preserved only if the rep-dependent rescaling is absorbed into the
   *definition* of the field's kinetic normalization. So the $\sqrt{C_2}$
   factor is recast as a non-canonical kinetic term that must be set
   by hand per representation — still a postulate, just relocated.

None of the three readings supplies a Wigner–Eckart derivation of the
seed off-diagonal. **The conjectured resolution of PAPER.md §6(a) is
ruled out by explicit calculation.**

## 5. Connection back to PAPER.md §7 conclusion

PAPER.md §7 lists five "next-derivable items"; this calculation bears
directly on item (iii):

> (iii) the explicit Wigner–Eckart computation of the
> $\langle \mathbf 1|T^a_{R'}|R\rangle$ reduced matrix element in the
> partial-compositeness UV completion of Caracciolo–Parolini–Serone, to
> test whether the $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ off-diagonal of Eq.~(1) is derivable
> as a single Clebsch coefficient (cf.\ Sec.~6(a)).

**Outcome.** The test is **negative**. Item (iii) is now **shown to be
unattainable** for two natural candidate parents:

- $R=2$ (doublet) from SU(3) fundamental: matrix element is **zero** by
  an SU(2) Wigner–Eckart selection rule ($1\otimes 1/2$ has no
  singlet). The doublet off-diagonal cannot be generated by any
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ *generator* in any parent rep — only by a
  spurion transforming as $j=1/2$, i.e.\ a Higgs-like doublet VEV.

- $R=3$ (triplet) from SO(4) bidoublet: matrix element of the unbroken
  generator is **zero** ($T_V|s\rangle=0$); matrix element of the
  broken (axial) generator is $3$, not the required $C_2(R=3)=2$. No
  SO(4) $(j,j)$ parent rep gives the right value.

**Honest status.** PAPER.md §7 item (iii) is **unattainable** as
stated — the $\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ off-diagonal is not a Wigner–Eckart
matrix element of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)_W$ generator in any
single-multiplet parent rep. The §6(a) language ("the residual
derivation gap is therefore a single reduced matrix element in a
partial-compositeness UV completion, not the whole seed structure")
should be **withdrawn or weakened**: the residual gap is not a single
Clebsch coefficient; it is a *coincidence between two independent
Wilson coefficients* at the UV scale.

**Updated honest status for PAPER.md §6(a):** the seed off-diagonal
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ remains an engineered Wilson coefficient with no
derivation from group theory in any natural parent-group embedding
tested. The mechanism that LAGRANGIAN.md §5 (L1, L3, L5) flagged as
saturating at $j(R)<\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ is the structural obstruction; this
calculation confirms that the Wigner–Eckart escape route does not exist.

**Items (i), (ii), (iv), (v) of PAPER.md §7 are unaffected by this
result.** Item (iii) should be replaced by an honest statement that the
$\sqrt{C_2(R)}$ off-diagonal is a structural feature of the seed
*Ansatz* that does not follow from any single-multiplet
Wigner–Eckart-style derivation.
