# Referee Report

## Summary

The signed-root algebra is exact and internally consistent.  The phenomenological
correction accurately maps the uncorrected Higgs and Fermi scales toward
`m_H` and `v/sqrt(2)` when normalized with the stated `M_Z`.  The proposed
operator has the correct leading custodial-breaking spurion dependence.

The central weakness is that no surveyed mainstream framework derives both the
negative-sector projector and the coefficient `C_F/C_A=3/8`.  The coefficient is
inserted as a Wilson coefficient or imported from unrelated Casimir language.

## A. Custodial SU(2)_L x SU(2)_R Field Theory

1. Is this mainstream physics?  Yes.
2. Does it have a Lagrangian or accepted EFT basis?  Yes: SM, SMEFT, HEFT,
   electroweak chiral Lagrangian.
3. Does it naturally produce `M_Z=M_W` in a custodial limit?  Yes, for `g' -> 0`
   at fixed `g` and `v`.
4. Does it naturally produce a correction proportional to `M_Z^2-M_W^2`?  Yes,
   as the leading hypercharge spurion.
5. Does it naturally produce `C_F/C_A=3/8`?  No.  Hypercharge gauges `T_R^3`,
   not the full custodial Casimir, and `C_A` is not selected by the tree-level
   vector splitting.
6. Does it act mainly on the tachyonic/negative-root sector?  No.  A separate
   negative-root multiplet must be introduced.
7. Does it preserve the positive W/Z pole branch?  It can by on-shell
   renormalization, but this is a convention.
8. What is assumed rather than derived?  The two-state negative sector and
   `kappa=3/8`.
9. PRD objection: the proposed operator is allowed, but its coefficient and
   field content are not derived from the stated symmetry.

## B. Composite Higgs / pNGB Higgs

1. Mainstream?  Yes.
2. Lagrangian/EFT?  Yes: `SO(5)/SO(4)` nonlinear sigma model and 5D/holographic
   completions.
3. Custodial limit?  Yes.
4. Proportional to vector splitting?  Plausible for hypercharge contributions.
5. Produces `3/8`?  No.  Gauge-potential coefficients are strong-sector
   spectral data.
6. Negative sector?  No.
7. Positive branch?  W/Z masses are fitted through `v`, `g`, and `g'`.
8. Assumptions?  Resonance data or Wilson coefficients tuned to the desired
   value.
9. PRD objection: pNGB symmetry does not know about the de Broglie-de Vries
   signed roots.

## C. Quiver / Deconstruction / Hidden Local Symmetry

1. Mainstream?  Yes.
2. Lagrangian/EFT?  Yes.
3. Custodial limit?  Yes in suitable moose models.
4. Proportional to vector splitting?  Possible through hypercharge or vector
   thresholds.
5. Produces `3/8`?  No.  Coefficients depend on site couplings and link-sector
   parameters.
6. Negative sector?  No, unless added.
7. Positive branch?  Yes after pole matching.
8. Assumptions?  Heavy-sector matching chosen to yield `3/8`.
9. PRD objection: continuous UV parameters make the numerical coefficient a fit.

## D. Supersymmetric D Terms

1. Mainstream?  Yes.
2. Lagrangian/EFT?  Yes.
3. Custodial limit?  Partly.  MSSM hypercharge and Yukawas break custodial
   symmetry; special Higgs sectors can improve it.
4. Proportional to vector splitting?  Yes, D terms give `g'^2 v^2`
   contributions.
5. Produces `3/8`?  No.  MSSM tree-level factors are set by charges and
   `tan beta`; extended D terms introduce tunable coefficients.
6. Negative sector?  Radiative EWSB has negative Higgs soft masses, but no
   de Broglie-de Vries two-root sector.
7. Positive branch?  W/Z pole masses are inputs.
8. Assumptions?  Projection onto the desired root and tuned D-term coefficient.
9. PRD objection: the proposed coefficient is not a SUSY prediction.

## E. Gauge-Theory Reggeization

1. Mainstream?  Yes for scattering amplitudes.
2. Lagrangian/EFT?  Yes: perturbative gauge theory and Regge-limit effective
   actions.
3. Custodial limit?  Not its natural organizing principle.
4. Proportional to vector splitting?  No.
5. Produces `3/8`?  It naturally produces `C_F/C_A` ratios in trajectory or
   impact-factor contexts, but not in this mass correction.
6. Negative sector?  No.
7. Positive branch?  No natural statement.
8. Assumptions?  Reinterpreting Regge intercept shifts as electroweak
   mass-square shifts.
9. PRD objection: wrong observable.  Reggeization concerns high-energy
   amplitudes, not Higgs tachyonic thresholds.

## F. String / D-Brane Models

1. Mainstream?  Mainstream as UV model building, not established phenomenology.
2. Lagrangian/EFT?  Yes after compactification, but model-specific.
3. Custodial limit?  Possible, not generic.
4. Proportional to vector splitting?  Only through the low-energy EFT.
5. Produces `3/8`?  No known universal mechanism.
6. Negative sector?  No.
7. Positive branch?  Low-energy matching can preserve it.
8. Assumptions?  Compactification data and threshold corrections selected to
   match the desired operator.
9. PRD objection: string theory adds no explanatory power unless a concrete
   compactification computes the coefficient.

## Overall Referee Judgment

The paper can claim an exact algebraic identity and a symmetry-motivated EFT
ansatz.  It should not claim a derivation.  The likely publishable version would
be a short note emphasizing the obstruction: custodial symmetry fixes the
spurion `M_Z^2-M_W^2`, while the coefficient `3/8` and the negative-root
projector remain phenomenological.

## Sister-Project Addendum

The imported sister project strengthens several objections:

- Custodial HEFT/SMEFT: `T_R^3` is a single-generator spurion.  The relevant
  trace ratios are `1/4`-type, not `C_F/C_A=3/8`.
- Composite Higgs: minimal `SO(5)/SO(4)` gauge potentials carry `3 g^2+g'^2`
  multiplicity and resonance integrals, not a universal `3/8`.
- SUSY D terms: hypercharge gives a `1/8` normalization and the `SU(2)_L`
  D-term survives in the custodial limit.
- Quiver models: `C_F/C_A` can be engineered by comparing scalar and vector
  representations, but the natural threshold is heavy-sector dependent.
- Reggeization: `C_F/C_A` is real in trajectory functions, but the observable is
  wrong for a Higgs tachyonic mass correction.
- Hidden-coupling note: the de Vries positive branch factorizes into a bare
  `3/8` times a dynamical de Broglie factor; removing that factor is not a
  custodial field-theory limit.

These additions do not change the final status.  They make the negative answer
more robust.

## Two-State Projector Addendum

The independent criticism of the minimal EFT is confirmed by the explicit
two-state scan.  The best mainstream route remains

```text
1/2 Psi_-^T [m_-^2 1 + c_Y g'^2 (H^\dagger H) sigma_3] Psi_-,
```

with `c_Y=3/16` fixed by matching.  This is a valid EFT operator, not a
prediction.

A two-state negative sector does not by itself rescue the proposal.  The most
general real perturbation is `a 1 + b sigma_3 + c sigma_1`; after imposing
trace preservation, the diagonal `sigma_3` form is simply the eigenbasis of an
arbitrary traceless matrix.  A referee would ask what fixes this basis to the
signed roots.

The stronger objection is group-theoretic.  Hypercharge is the gauged
`T_R^3` spurion.  The coefficient `3/8` uses the full fundamental Casimir
`C_F=3/4`, but a single-generator spurion at order `g'^2` supplies
`(T_R^3)^2=1/4` in the fundamental.  Unless a UV calculation restores the full
Casimir in a custodial-breaking effect, the coefficient is inserted rather than
derived.

## Loop-Size Addendum

The proposed correction is `epsilon=(3/32)g'^2 v^2`, so it is tree-size.  A
standard one-loop self-energy

```text
delta m^2 = A g'^2 C v^2/(16 pi^2)
```

would need `A=2 pi^2` with the full fundamental Casimir or `A=6 pi^2` with the
single hypercharge generator.  These are not natural weakly-coupled finite
parts.  Heavy thresholds and D terms can be large enough, but then the result
depends on threshold masses, charges, and signs.  This reinforces the Wilson
coefficient objection.

## GUT-Normalization Addendum

The equality between `3/8` and the minimal-`SU(5)` tree-level weak-angle value
does not help the proposal.  In `SU(5)`, `3/8` comes from the hypercharge
normalization quotient `(3/5)/(1+3/5)` at a high scale.  The proposed mass
correction uses the low-energy `SU(2)` Casimir quotient `(3/4)/2`.  A referee
would treat these as unrelated appearances of the same rational unless a full
GUT threshold calculation maps one into the other while also producing the
two-state negative-root sector.

## Paired D-Term Addendum

A paired exotic scalar model is a useful boundary case.  It is mainstream as a
Lagrangian and gives an exactly traceless split:

```text
V_D = g'^2/2 (q_H |H|^2 + q_S |S_+|^2 - q_S |S_-|^2)^2.
```

For `q_H=1/2`, the coefficient is simply `kappa=q_S`.  Setting `q_S=3/8`
reproduces the proposed correction, but this is an assigned exotic
hypercharge, not a consequence of `C_F/C_A`.  A skeptical referee would also
object that `SU(2)_L` singlets with `Y=+/-3/8` are fractionally electrically
charged, while doublets have fractional components.  Extra confinement or decay
structure would be needed and would be model-dependent.

## Charge-Quantization Addendum

The charge-lattice check makes the paired D-term objection sharper.  Ordinary
SM and minimal-`SU(5)` hypercharges lie on a `1/6` lattice, while
`(3/8)/(1/6)=9/4`.  Therefore the only explicit D-term model found so far uses
hypercharges outside the usual quantized lattice.  This is allowed in a
bottom-up vectorlike exotic sector, but not a natural derivation of the
coefficient.

## Independent-Criticism Addendum

A second independent criticism pass found no overlooked natural route.  The
remaining possible mechanisms all repackage the same step: either replace the
single hypercharge generator by the full `SU(2)` Casimir, or choose a
charge/Wilson coefficient equal to `3/8`.  The paired D-term construction is
therefore the strongest boundary case, but it changes only the status of the
operator form, not the status of the coefficient.

## SU(5) Representation Addendum

Scanning ordinary `SU(5)` tensor weights through rank 8 confirms the simpler
lattice argument: weights generated by `-1/3` and `+1/2` remain on a `1/6`
lattice, so `Y=3/8` is absent.  A larger ordinary `SU(5)` representation does
not naturally provide the charge needed by the paired D-term model.

## Conditional No-Go Addendum

The project now has a conditional no-go statement.  Assuming the ordinary SM
hypercharge normalization and custodial breaking by the single generator
`T_R^3`, the leading correction can be written with an arbitrary `kappa`, but
there is no mainstream symmetry reason for `kappa=C_F/C_A`.  A derivation must
therefore compute a UV threshold or change the charge lattice.  Neither has
been found.

## Custodial Representation Addendum

The direct custodial embedding `Y=T_R^3+X` also fails to supply the paired
D-term charge naturally.  Scanning `SU(2)_R` weights through `j_R=4` shows that
`X=3/8-T_R^3` is never on the ordinary `1/6` charge lattice.  This closes the
simplest composite/custodial charge-assignment loophole.

## Kinetic-Mixing Addendum

Hidden-`U(1)` kinetic mixing can generate effective nonstandard charges and is
mainstream.  However, the required `3/8` then comes from a continuous mixing
parameter `epsilon(g_X/g_Y)q_X`, not from custodial `SU(2)` group theory.  This
is another engineering route, not a prediction.

## LEE Addendum

The sister project's `p_LEE=1.564e-6` result changes the rhetorical posture but
not the field-theory conclusion.  A referee should not dismiss the closed-form
mass relation as low-effort numerology if that look-elsewhere analysis is
accepted.  However, statistical surprise is not a Lagrangian derivation.  The
coefficient/projector problem remains exactly where it was: hypercharge gives a
single-generator spurion, while the proposed coefficient uses the full
`SU(2)` Casimir ratio.

## Scheme-Dependence Addendum

The sister scheme analysis also improves the phenomenological framing.  The
`M_F` residual is about `25 GeV^2`, roughly `2%` of the usual one-loop
electroweak `Delta r * v^2/2` scale.  A referee should therefore not overstate
the residual trace as fatal.  But because no canonical `G_F`, on-shell, or
MS-bar vev definition hits the corrected value, this remains a plausible
radiative-size residue rather than a derived correction.
